# Scars of the Primordial War

> *“Some battlefields are buried. Others continue happening.”*  
> —[[Professor Phineas Phantomhive II]]

The **Scars of the Primordial War** are places where [[The Primordial War]] damaged time, space, memory, or reality itself. They are not ordinary ruins, nor are they simply sites where old magic remains. They are wounds in the world's structure.

Some scars are visible: glasslike craters, forests where shadows fall in the wrong direction, roads that return travelers before they left, or towers whose upper floors exist several minutes ahead of their foundations.

Others are subtler. A village may repeat the same festival every winter without realizing it has done so for centuries. A battlefield may still hear commands from armies that never reached it. A river may remember a course it has not followed since before recorded history.

## Nature of the Scars

The Primordial War was not fought only across land and sea. Its greatest violence struck the rules beneath the world. Time buckled. Distance folded. Causality cracked. In some places, the world healed badly.

Common scar effects include:

- **time loops**, ranging from a few heartbeats to entire repeating seasons
- **spatial folds**, where two distant places overlap
- **delayed ruins**, whose collapse happens one stone at a time across centuries
- **memory bleed**, where people recall lives, wars, or deaths that were never theirs
- **age storms**, which make objects or creatures grow older or younger without pattern
- **mirror wounds**, where reflective surfaces open toward [[The Mirrorgate]]
- **chronal ghosts**, echoes of people caught in a moment that refuses to end

## Connection to the Mirrorgate

The scars and [[The Mirrorgate]] are inseparable in most serious scholarship. One theory claims the Mirrorgate was born when the Primordial War tore a path through time and space. Another claims the Mirrorgate already existed, and the war merely widened its doors until the damage spread into Zerkalo.

Either way, many scars behave like shallow Mirrorgate crossings. They may not allow full travel between worlds, but they let moments, reflections, identities, and impossible distances leak through.

## Recognizing a Scar

Use these checks when characters investigate a possible war-scar:

- **DC 12 Perception:** Notice a repeated sound, shadow, reflection, or motion.
- **DC 13 Investigation:** Find physical evidence that two different times or locations overlap.
- **DC 14 Arcana:** Detect temporal or spatial distortion rather than ordinary spell residue.
- **DC 15 History:** Connect the site to a mythic account of the Primordial War.
- **DC 16 Insight:** Realize a memory, instinct, or emotion in the area does not belong to the character experiencing it.
- **DC 17 Survival:** Notice that paths, distances, or landmarks cannot be mapped consistently.

> [!warning]
> A scar should rarely be solved by a single check. Let successful checks reveal how the place is wrong, not immediately how to fix it.

## Adventure Use

- A town has lived the same week for three generations and executes anyone who notices.
- A ruined bridge crosses a river that will not exist until tomorrow.
- A battlefield still contains soldiers from several incompatible versions of history.
- A noble house built its estate over a spatial fold and now has rooms no architect designed.
- A mirror-scar opens into the Mirrorgate whenever someone speaks a name that history erased.
