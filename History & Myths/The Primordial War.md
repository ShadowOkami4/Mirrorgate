# The Primordial War

> *“Every culture claims the first war began for a different reason. This is understandable. None of them were there, and the gods who were have been notably unavailable for interview.”*  
> —[[Professor Phineas Phantomhive II]]

Before the present age of [[Zerkalo]], before its kingdoms drew borders and before most peoples preserved reliable histories, the world was said to have been scarred by a conflict now remembered only through myth.

Different traditions give this catastrophe different names: **the Primordial War**, **the War of Tides**, **the War Before Dawn**, **the War Beyond Hours**, or simply **the First Breaking**.

All accounts agree on one point: the war was not a mortal conflict.

## War of the Lost Gods

The oldest legends describe powers whose true names have been erased from the world. These beings are sometimes called the **Prime Gods**, **Old Makers**, or **First Divinities**. Their servants included celestials, fiends, spirits, forged soldiers, and stranger hosts that modern scholars cannot comfortably classify.

The war was not fought for a city, throne, or border. It was a struggle over the shape of reality itself. Mountains were raised and shattered as siegeworks. Seas withdrew from one coast and drowned another. Time was bent into weapons, distance was folded into traps, and entire battlefields were hidden between one second and the next. Some myths claim that, for a time, no ordinary life could endure upon Zerkalo at all.

Whether this is literal truth or poetic exaggeration remains debated.

## The God Who Ended It

The war ended when one divinity, whose name is now either forgotten or deliberately hidden, brought the conflict to a close. The victory was not clean. The surviving legends insist that the god ended the war by destroying the warring Prime Gods—and themselves along with them.

Their bodies vanished.

Their power did not.

Instead, the violence of their ending tore through time and space. The world survived, but not cleanly. Places where the war struck hardest became [[Scars of the Primordial War]]: wounds where distance, memory, chronology, and reflection no longer obey the ordinary laws of Zerkalo.

## Divine Echoes

Some religions teach that the Old Gods are gone forever. Others claim their essences still whisper through dreams, bloodlines, natural disasters, and places where time itself remembers the war.

One persistent belief speaks of **divine avatars**: living echoes created to preserve balance, continue the war, or finish unfinished divine designs. No consensus exists on whether these avatars are guardians, weapons, prisoners, or lies invented by cults hungry for power.

> [!question] Scholar's Problem
> If the gods were destroyed, why do some places still behave as though the war is waiting to continue?

## Adventure Use

- A ruin contains murals showing the same god as both savior and betrayer.
- A battlefield repeats the final hour of a war no mortal army remembers.
- A cult believes the Primordial War never ended and seeks to awaken its final battlefield.
- A noble house claims descent from a divine avatar and demands recognition.
