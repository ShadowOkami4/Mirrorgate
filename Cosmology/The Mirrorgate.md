# The Mirrorgate

> *“A mirror is a door pretending to be furniture.”*  
> —[[Professor Phineas Phantomhive II]]

The **Mirrorgate** is not a country, a road, or a conventional plane. It is a distorted threshold between realities: a place of reflection, misremembered time, broken identity, folded distance, and impossible passage.

Most people see a mirror and expect only their own face.

Those with the wrong magic, the wrong blood, or the wrong invitation may see a way through.

## A Door Between Time and Space

With precise arcane practice, certain mirrors can be used as gates into the Mirrorgate. These crossings are unstable and rarely safe. Distance, direction, and chronology cannot be trusted once the traveler passes beyond the surface.

Within the Mirrorgate, a corridor may lead into yesterday, a ballroom may open onto a battlefield from another world, and a person's reflection may know something the person has forgotten.

## Origin Theories

No accepted origin exists. The most widely repeated theory links the Mirrorgate directly to [[The Primordial War]], claiming that the conflict fractured time and space so violently that the wound echoed across countless reflections.

Other scholars argue that the Mirrorgate is older than the war and that the gods merely damaged something they never created. In either case, the connection is extreme enough that serious scholars no longer study the Mirrorgate separately from the [[Scars of the Primordial War]].

Both explanations are distressing.

## The Phantomhive Recurrence

One of the strangest patterns in Mirrorgate research is the repeated appearance of the name **Phantomhive** across incompatible realities and timelines.

In one reflection, the name belongs to wandering nobility. In another, it is tied to an academy of magic and science. In the Library of Kagami, it belongs most prominently to [[Professor Phineas Phantomhive II]].

Whether this recurrence is coincidence, curse, bloodline anomaly, or deliberate design remains unknown.

## Distorted Inhabitants

Creatures trapped within the Mirrorgate may lose their original shape, memory, or selfhood. Some become reflections of what they once were; others become hungry fragments seeking a life to imitate.

The most commonly catalogued examples are known as [[Shard Monsters]].

## Signs of Mirrorgate Influence

- mirrors showing a delayed or incorrect reflection
- glass surfaces rippling like water
- familiar rooms arranged in impossible layouts
- repeated names, faces, or events across unrelated places
- memories that do not belong to the person remembering them
- roads that arrive before they depart
- rooms where seconds pass in the wrong order
- ruins visibly scarred by battles that happened outside normal history
- creatures whose bodies resemble broken glass, shadow, or reflected light

## Adventure Use

- A mirror in a noble estate opens only during thunderstorms.
- A character's reflection warns them of a choice they have not yet made.
- A lost person returns from the Mirrorgate aged only one hour, while ten years passed outside.
- A Phantomhive ancestor appears in a reflection and denies ever dying.
