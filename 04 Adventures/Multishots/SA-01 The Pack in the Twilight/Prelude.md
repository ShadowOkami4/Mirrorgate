# Prelude: The Pack in the Twilight

> [!info] Adventure Information
> **Format:** Few-shot adventure  
> **Starting Level:** 4  
> **Difficulty:** Not yet determined

## Where Fate Gathers

There are meetings arranged by kings, meetings demanded by war, and meetings born from simple chance.

This was none of those.

Each adventurer received the same letter: a few carefully chosen words written in dark ink, bearing neither royal seal nor promise of reward. Its author, [[Sandra Mistic]], asked only that they come to her inn within the [[Forest of Thieves]]. She offered no explanation for how she knew their names, where her messenger had found them, or why she believed they would answer.

Nevertheless, they came.

From distant roads and different lives, the adventurers arrive at the same lonely clearing beneath the dying light of day. Most have never met. None yet knows why they were chosen. But as twilight settles between the trees and the paths behind them vanish into shadow, one truth becomes difficult to ignore:

Something has drawn them here.

And fate has already begun to close its jaws.

> [!quote] Read Aloud
> The last light of evening lies broken across the forest floor. Ancient trees crowd around a small clearing, their branches woven so tightly overhead that the sky appears only in narrow shards of violet and gold.
>
> One by one, strangers emerge from separate paths. Each carries the same letter. Each bears the same destination.
>
> Somewhere beyond the trees, unseen in the gathering dark, a wolf begins to howl.

Allow the characters time to introduce themselves, compare their letters, and decide whether to travel together. The forest paths offer no clear alternative, and remaining alone after nightfall would be unwise.

### Examining the Letters

The letters appear identical at first glance. Characters who examine them can learn the following:

- **DC 10 Intelligence (Investigation):** The ink is unnaturally fresh, regardless of when or where each letter was received.
- **DC 12 Wisdom (Perception):** A faint watermark depicts a closed eye surrounded by thornlike lines.
- **DC 14 Intelligence (Arcana):** Each letter carries a weak divination aura. The magic did not merely deliver the message—it helped determine its recipient.
- **DC 15 Wisdom (Insight):** Although the wording is polite, the author was frightened and took great care not to reveal too much in writing.

On a failed check, a character still notices that the letter is unusually well preserved. Failure should obscure additional details, not prevent the party from following the invitation.

## 00/B1 — The Forest and the Thieves

The path winds deeper into the [[Forest of Thieves]], where roots break through the earth like the bones of some buried giant. The air grows colder as the party travels, and familiar woodland sounds gradually fade. No birds call from the canopy. No insects stir beneath the leaves.

Instead, the adventurers encounter clusters of pale, luminous plants growing beside the trail.

> [!quote] Read Aloud
> Delicate stems rise from the black soil, crowned by translucent leaves that glow with a faint blue light. They bend toward you as you pass—not toward the sun, but toward the warmth of living bodies.
>
> Their light pulses once, slowly, like the beat of a sleeping heart.

### The Luminous Plants

The plants cannot be identified by ordinary magical or scholarly knowledge. Examining them reveals different pieces of the mystery:

- **DC 10 Wisdom (Perception):** The plants lean toward warm-blooded creatures and recoil slightly from undead beings or necrotic magic.
- **DC 12 Intelligence (Investigation):** Their roots do not grow naturally through the soil. They emerge from thin, glasslike cracks beneath the earth.
- **DC 13 Intelligence (Nature):** The plants share no recognizable traits with the native flora of Zerkalo.
- **DC 14 Intelligence (Arcana):** They radiate faint conjuration magic mixed with an unfamiliar planar resonance.
- **DC 15 Wisdom (Survival):** The plants do not belong in this forest. Their growth suggests that they may not belong anywhere in this world—or even upon this plane of existence.

A character who touches a plant feels a momentary chill and hears a distant whisper in an unknown language. The plant then closes its leaves and becomes dormant for one minute. A successful **DC 13 Intelligence (Arcana) check** confirms that this reaction is magical communication rather than poison or telepathy.

> [!tip] Essential Clue
> The party should learn that the plants are unnatural even if every check fails. Successful checks reveal how unnatural they are and foreshadow their planar origin.

The party has little time to investigate. Voices soon carry through the trees, followed by the glow of hooded lanterns. A band of thieves blocks the road ahead, watching for travelers caught beyond the safety of civilization.

### Detecting the Ambush

Compare the thieves' Dexterity (Stealth) result against each character's passive Wisdom (Perception). Characters actively watching the road may instead attempt a **DC 13 Wisdom (Perception) check**.

On a success, a character notices one of the following before the thieves reveal themselves:

- A lantern briefly covered beneath a dark cloak
- Fresh bootprints beside the road
- A bowstring drawn between the trees
- Several voices falling silent at once

If at least one character detects the ambush, the party cannot be surprised and gains a moment to hide, prepare, or address the thieves first.

The characters may confront the thieves or attempt to slip past them. To avoid notice, the party must succeed on a **DC 12 group Dexterity (Stealth) check**. Characters who discovered the ambush before being seen make this check with advantage. If the group fails, the thieves spot them and move to intercept.

### Dealing with the Thieves

The thieves initially demand coin, food, or valuables rather than immediately attacking. The characters may attempt to change their minds:

- **DC 12 Charisma (Persuasion):** Convince the thieves that the party carries little worth stealing or that letting them pass is the safer choice.
- **DC 13 Charisma (Deception):** Claim that armed reinforcements are following close behind.
- **DC 14 Charisma (Intimidation):** Frighten the thieves into abandoning the road.
- **DC 12 Wisdom (Insight):** Recognize that the thieves are anxious and keep watching the deeper forest. They fear something nearby more than they fear the adventurers.

If questioned peacefully, a thief admits that strange lights have appeared between the trees and that several members of their band vanished during the past week. A successful **DC 13 Charisma (Persuasion or Intimidation) check** reveals that the missing thieves were last seen traveling north.

> [!danger] Encounter
> If negotiation or intimidation fails, roll initiative.

Whatever the outcome, the encounter should leave the characters with the sense that they are being observed. A snapped branch, a distant silhouette, or another low howl may suggest that the thieves are not the most dangerous hunters in these woods.

A character who searches the surrounding forest after the encounter can attempt a **DC 14 Wisdom (Survival) check**. On a success, they find tracks belonging to a large wolf circling the area. The tracks occasionally disappear for several paces before resuming, as though the creature passed through the shadows themselves.

## 00/E1 — The Lady at the Lonely Inn

Beyond the thieves, the southern path descends into a sheltered hollow. There, warm light shines between the trees.

At its center stands [[To the Eternal Lady]], an elegant inn impossibly untouched by the surrounding wilderness. Its windows glow amber against the night. Music and laughter drift from within, offering a welcome so perfect that it almost feels rehearsed.

Local thieves speak of the place as if it has always been here. Older travelers disagree. Some claim the same inn once stood in another forest entirely, and a few stranger tales insist it has appeared beneath skies that did not belong to Zerkalo.

> [!quote] Read Aloud
> The door opens upon polished wood, golden lamplight, and the welcome heat of a crowded hearth. Travelers drink beneath hanging lanterns while dice tumble across gaming tables. The scent of roasted meat and spiced ale fills the room.
>
> Behind the counter, a finely dressed tiefling woman calmly polishes a glass. Her eyes pass over each of you in turn before settling upon the letters in your hands.
>
> “At last,” she says.

The woman introduces herself as Madam [[Sandra Mistic]], proprietor of the inn. She welcomes the adventurers warmly, offers them food and lodging, and behaves as though their arrival were expected to the hour.

Sandra is genuinely friendly, not merely polite. She wants the inn to feel safe and ordinary, and she works very hard to hide any sign that she is anything other than a gracious tiefling tavern keeper.

### First Impressions

Characters observing the common room may make the following checks:

- **DC 11 Wisdom (Perception):** No mud, leaves, or water mark the floor despite the inn's woodland location and crowded clientele.
- **DC 12 Intelligence (Investigation):** Several patrons repeat small actions at irregular intervals—a shuffled hand of cards, a raised cup, or the same unfinished sentence.
- **DC 13 Wisdom (Insight):** The guests appear genuinely cheerful, but their emotions feel strangely shallow, as if they are playing familiar roles.
- **DC 14 Intelligence (Arcana):** Powerful illusion and abjuration magic permeate the building. The inn is protected, concealed, and larger within than its exterior should allow.
- **DC 15 Wisdom (Perception):** Sandra never breathes, and no condensation forms when she speaks near a freshly polished glass.
- **DC 16 Intelligence (Arcana):** The building carries traces of planar displacement. It has not merely been hidden; at some point, it has been moved.
- **DC 17 Intelligence (History):** A carved mark on one beam resembles a place-name from no known Zerkalo record.

The patrons are harmless magical echoes created to make the inn feel inhabited. Sandra does not volunteer this fact, but she admits it if confronted privately. She created them because years of solitary vigilance became unbearable.

> [!warning] Tavern Violence
> Sandra tolerates fear, confusion, and desperate mistakes. She does not tolerate senseless violence in her tavern. If characters start a destructive fight without cause and ignore clear warnings to stop, Sandra intervenes first with containment magic. If they continue, she treats them as threats to the inn and possible fuel for her phylactery.

If the characters present the letters, Sandra's practiced smile briefly falters. The merriment of the common room continues around them, but her manner becomes guarded and solemn. She asks the party to follow her somewhere private, refusing to discuss the matter where others might overhear.

Any character watching Sandra may attempt a **DC 12 Wisdom (Insight) check**. On a success, they recognize fear rather than hostility. On a result of 16 or higher, they also notice that Sandra is not afraid of the party—she is afraid of what their arrival confirms.

She leads them through a locked door and down a narrow passage beneath the inn.

## 01 — The Lich and Her Vigil

The chamber below bears no resemblance to the comfort above. It is part shrine, part study, and part tomb. Candles burn around an ancient altar. Shelves sag beneath forbidden volumes, preserved remains, and relics wrapped in silver chains. Bones have been arranged into protective circles across the stone floor.

Here, Sandra allows her disguise to fall.

> [!quote] Read Aloud
> The tiefling's living features fade like paint washed from glass. Warm skin withers against bone. The light in her eyes becomes a cold and deathless flame.
>
> A lich stands before you.
>
> Yet she does not raise a hand to strike. Instead, she steps carefully behind the altar, placing stone and candlelight between herself and your weapons.
>
> “I understand,” she says softly, “that this requires an explanation.”

Sandra does not threaten the party. She appears cautious, weary, and deeply saddened by what she must reveal. She admits that she is a lich, but insists that she did not embrace undeath for conquest, vanity, or personal power. She accepted lichdom as the price of guarding the artifact until her final death.

### Reading the Chamber

Before or during Sandra's explanation, the characters may examine the chamber:

- **DC 10 Wisdom (Medicine):** The bones are from several humanoid species, but none show signs of violence. Most appear ancient.
- **DC 12 Intelligence (Religion):** The arrangement resembles a funerary vigil rather than an altar of sacrifice.
- **DC 13 Intelligence (Investigation):** The silver chains and protective circles are positioned to contain something beneath the altar, not Sandra.
- **DC 14 Intelligence (Arcana):** Multiple layers of abjuration magic reinforce the chamber. Several wards are weakening under pressure from an outside force.
- **DC 15 Wisdom (Perception):** A nearly inaudible heartbeat comes from beneath the altar, though whatever produces it beats only once each minute.

A character can attempt a **DC 13 Wisdom (Insight) check** while Sandra explains herself. On a success, the character believes she is sincere about wishing the party no harm. On a result of 17 or higher, the character realizes that Sandra is withholding one fact: she knows more about the artifact's connection to the party than she is willing to reveal.

She remains at the inn because she is the guardian of an artifact that came from beyond this world. Sandra is not native to Zerkalo either. She came from another world and accepted undeath so the artifact would never be left unguarded. Its nature makes it too dangerous to abandon, and the magic binding Sandra to its protection prevents her from leaving. For years, she has kept watch while the world beyond her doors continued without her.

Recently, a plea for aid reached her from an old friend. Sandra cannot answer it herself, so she sent enchanted letters in search of those whom fate—or the artifact—judged capable of acting in her place.

She places an old map upon the altar. It marks a ruined site in the northern reaches of the forest.

> [!quote] Read Aloud
> Sandra smooths the map with one skeletal hand.
>
> “I did not choose you,” she says. “That is the part which troubles me.”
>
> Her cold gaze passes from one adventurer to the next.
>
> “But something did.”

Sandra asks the party to travel to the ruin, find her friend, and discover why the call for help was sent. She cannot promise what waits there, only that the danger is real—and that the strange growth spreading through the forest may be connected.

### Questioning Sandra

Sandra answers reasonable questions honestly, except where doing so might reveal the artifact's full nature. Checks can uncover additional information:

- **DC 12 Charisma (Persuasion):** Sandra reveals that her friend was studying the luminous plants before sending the plea for aid.
- **DC 13 Wisdom (Insight):** Her fear intensified when she saw the party together. Their arrival fulfills part of a warning she received long ago.
- **DC 14 Intelligence (Investigation), while studying the map:** Several landmarks have been added in different inks across many decades. One recent mark resembles the watermark on the letters.
- **DC 15 Intelligence (Arcana):** The map is enchanted to reveal the safest available path, but its route changes whenever the planar disturbance grows stronger.
- **DC 16 Charisma (Persuasion):** Sandra admits that the artifact reacted to each adventurer before she sent the letters. She does not know why it chose them.
- **DC 16 Wisdom (Insight):** Sandra is ashamed of her lichdom, not proud of it. She fears being judged as a monster more than she fears being attacked.

Sandra refuses to open the sealed space beneath the altar. No successful check can force her to endanger the inn and the surrounding forest, though exceptional roleplay may persuade her to explain more in a later chapter.

> [!tip] Running Ability Checks
> Do not require checks for information necessary to continue the adventure. The party always receives the map, the location of the ruins, and Sandra's request. Ability checks provide context, foreshadowing, tactical advantages, or reasons to trust—or distrust—what they have been told.

The adventurers entered the woods as strangers, each following a letter they did not understand. If they accept Sandra's request, they leave the inn with a common purpose.

For the first time, they travel not as individuals, but as a pack.

And beyond the reach of the inn's warm light, something in the twilight is waiting for them.
