# The Mirrorgate

> *"A mirror is a door pretending to be furniture."*  
> —[[Professor Phineas Phantomhive II]]

The **Mirrorgate** is the world behind the glass: a plane of broken land, reflected places, and unreliable time, lying just on the other side of every mirror. It is neither empty nor dead. Rivers run, weather turns, forests grow, and creatures that have never seen the material world make their homes there.

It is not whole, either. The Mirrorgate is a realm in pieces—countless shards of land drifting through a luminous sky, each one a reflection of somewhere else.

Most people see a mirror and expect only their own face. Those with the wrong magic, the wrong blood, or the wrong invitation may see a way through.

## The Shardlands

Travelers who return from the Mirrorgate describe its land the same way: as a shattered country. The ground is broken into **shards**—floating islands of earth and stone ranging from boulders to plains wide enough to hold a city. They hang at every angle, turning slowly, and each keeps its own sense of *down*. A traveler can walk over the edge of one shard and find themselves standing on its underside.

The space between the shards is called **the Drift**. It is not a void. It is full of pale mist, tumbling stones, arcing rivers, and flocks of creatures migrating from shard to shard. Water in the Drift falls toward whichever shard is nearest, so rivers leap from one island to the next, and waterfalls often pour *upward* into the shard above.

There is no sun. Light comes from the sky itself, which looks like an enormous cracked mirror: each fragment shows a different sky from somewhere else—noon beside midnight, a thunderstorm beside a clear dawn. Weather is borrowed the same way. It can rain on one shard while the next lies under snow.

## Reflected Places

The shards are not random. Each one is a reflection of a real place, and a traveler who knows the original will usually recognize it: a familiar hall, street, or hillside, broken off at the edges and hanging in the sky.

How faithfully a shard resembles its original depends on **mirror memory**—how long, and how steadily, that place has been reflected.

- **Deep reflections.** A mirror that has hung in the same room for centuries produces a near-perfect copy on the other side: the same furniture, the same cracks in the plaster, the same view from the window. Great houses, old temples, and palaces with famous mirrors have some of the most faithful reflections in the Mirrorgate.
- **Shallow reflections.** A place reflected only briefly—in a hand mirror, a puddle, a polished shield—becomes a blurred, dreamlike shard. Rooms are the wrong size, doors lead to the wrong places, and details dissolve under close inspection.
- **Still water.** Lakes and ponds are the oldest mirrors of all. Much of the Mirrorgate's wilderness is reflected from them, which is why its forests so often hang upside down beneath the shards, growing toward a sky that is really the bottom of a lake.
- **The unreflected.** Places that have never been reflected do not appear at all. Caves, sealed vaults, and the hearts of mountains leave holes in the Shardlands.

Every reflected place is also *reversed*. Writing runs backward, familiar streets turn the wrong way, and doors open on the wrong side. Experienced travelers learn to read signs in a mirror—or carry one.

Not every shard reflects [[Zerkalo]]. The Mirrorgate touches other worlds too, and some shards show cities, skies, and landscapes that no Zerkalo map records.

## Time Behind the Glass

Time in the Mirrorgate mostly works. A traveler who spends an afternoon there usually returns an afternoon later. But the plane's hold on time is loose, and in places it slips:

- a shard where it has been the same evening for three hundred years, and the festival lanterns never burn out
- a stretch of the Drift where a traveler ages a week in an hour
- rooms where seconds happen in the wrong order
- a waterfall that keeps falling upward through the same few moments

The deeper and older a reflection, the more closely its time follows the original. Shallow reflections drift, and some have come loose entirely.

## Life Behind the Glass

The Mirrorgate is inhabited. Alongside the [[Shardkin]]—creatures made from broken reflections—it has plants, animals, and perhaps even peoples of its own, which were born there and have never known another world. See [[Life in the Mirrorgate]].

## Crossing Over

With precise arcane practice, certain mirrors can be used as gates into the Mirrorgate. A crossing usually leads into the reflection of the mirror's own surroundings: step through a mirror in a noble estate and you arrive in that estate's reflection, hanging somewhere in the Shardlands. From there, a traveler can cross the Drift to other shards—and, through the mirrors on those shards, return to places far from where they began.

These crossings are unstable and rarely safe. Distance, direction, and chronology cannot be trusted once the traveler passes beyond the surface.

## Origin Theories

No accepted origin exists. The most widely repeated theory links the Mirrorgate directly to [[The Primordial War]], claiming that the conflict fractured time and space so violently that the wound echoed across countless reflections. If so, the Shardlands are exactly what they look like: a world that was broken and never put back together.

Other scholars argue that the Mirrorgate is older than the war and that the gods merely shattered something they never created. In either case, the connection is strong enough that serious scholars no longer study the Mirrorgate separately from the [[Scars of the Primordial War]].

Both explanations are distressing.

## The Phantomhive Recurrence

One of the strangest patterns in Mirrorgate research is the repeated appearance of the name **Phantomhive** across incompatible realities and timelines.

In one reflection, the name belongs to wandering nobility. In another, it is tied to an academy of magic and science. In the Library of Kagami, it belongs most prominently to [[Professor Phineas Phantomhive II]].

Whether this recurrence is coincidence, curse, bloodline anomaly, or deliberate design remains unknown.

## Signs of Mirrorgate Influence

- mirrors showing a delayed or incorrect reflection
- glass surfaces rippling like water
- familiar rooms arranged in impossible layouts
- repeated names, faces, or events across unrelated places
- memories that do not belong to the person remembering them
- roads that arrive before they depart
- rooms where seconds pass in the wrong order
- ruins visibly scarred by battles that happened outside normal history
- creatures whose bodies resemble broken glass, shadow, or reflected light

> [!gm]- Known Place Within the Mirrorgate (SA-01 spoiler)
> The [[Sanctum of the Flower in the Mirror]] is a ruined temple caught within the Mirrorgate. It is the interstitial counterpart of the [[Temple of the Moon in Water]] in the [[Forest of Thieves]]: two manifestations of one location divided between Zerkalo and the plane between time and space. Unlike an ordinary reflection, the sanctum is not a copy—it is the missing half of the temple.
