# Madam Sandra Mistic

> *“Welcome, dear. Sit, warm yourself, and do not start a fight in my house. I have so few rules left, and I dislike making examples of people.”*

## NPC Summary

| Detail | Information |
|---|---|
| **Full Name** | Sandra Mistic |
| **Known As** | Madam Mistic; the Eternal Lady |
| **Species** | Tiefling from another world |
| **True Nature** | Lich by oath, not ambition |
| **Occupation** | Innkeeper and guardian |

| **Adventure Role** | Patron, keeper of forbidden knowledge, and possible ally |

## Overview

Madam Sandra Mistic is the enigmatic proprietor of [[To the Eternal Lady]], a secluded inn currently hidden within the [[Forest of Thieves]]. To ordinary travelers, she appears to be an elegant tiefling innkeeper: warm, composed, observant, and unfailingly courteous. She greets guests with genuine hospitality, remembers their preferred drinks with unsettling accuracy, and works very hard to seem like nothing more dangerous than the owner of a lonely roadside tavern.

Beneath that living disguise, however, Sandra is an ancient lich.

Sandra is not native to [[Zerkalo]]. She came from another world, carrying—or pursuing—an artifact of such terrible power that a single mortal lifetime was not enough to guard it. She did not embrace lichdom for dominion, immortality, vanity, or limitless arcane power. She accepted undeath as a sentence and a promise: to protect the artifact until her final death, no matter how long the vigil demanded.

The inn itself may not be native to Zerkalo either. It does not always remain in the same forest, and some accounts suggest it has crossed between worlds. Sandra is bound to it, but whether she guides its wandering or merely endures it is unclear.

That distinction matters to her. Sandra hates being mistaken for the sort of lich who builds towers from bones and calls it ambition. She sees herself as a jailer, innkeeper, and last line of defense. The fact that her methods are frightening does not make her cruel, at least not in her own eyes.

Her long guardianship has made Sandra patient and knowledgeable, but also deeply isolated. The lively guests within her inn are magical echoes created to soften the silence of an existence spent watching, waiting, and fearing the day her wards finally fail.

> [!warning] House Rule of the Eternal Lady
> Sandra is friendly, but her tavern is not a place for senseless violence. A brawl caused by panic, self-defense, or misunderstanding may earn a warning. A deliberate tavern fight without cause, mercy, or anyone willing to stop it may earn something far worse. Those who turn her refuge into a slaughterhouse risk becoming the next corpse claimed for her phylactery.

## Appearance

### Living Disguise

Sandra presents herself as a poised tiefling woman dressed in refined but practical clothing suited to a prosperous innkeeper. Her movements are deliberate, her clothing immaculate, and her expression carefully controlled. She smiles often, laughs softly at harmless jokes, and tries very hard to appear alive in all the little ways guests expect.

She is often found behind the counter polishing a glass that was never dirty.

Small details betray her true nature:

- She does not breathe unless she remembers to imitate it.
- Her body produces no warmth.
- She rarely eats or drinks in front of guests.
- Her eyes linger on entrances, shadows, and unfamiliar magic.
- Her smile fades the moment she believes no one is watching.

### True Form

When Sandra releases her disguise, her living features wither away to reveal an undead body sustained by powerful necromancy. Cold light burns within her empty eyes, and her voice gains a faint echo, as though another distant voice speaks alongside her.

Her true form is frightening, but not feral or decayed through neglect. Sandra carries herself with the same dignity in death that she displays in disguise. If anything, the reveal embarrasses her more than it empowers her. She does not enjoy being seen as a monster, even when she is fully capable of behaving like one.

## Personality

Sandra is polite, restrained, and genuinely friendly. Her kindness is not an act, though the living mask she wears certainly is. She likes guests, conversation, music, warm meals, and the fragile illusion that her tavern is an ordinary place where tired people can rest.

She prefers negotiation over violence and dislikes unnecessary displays of power. Even when threatened, she responds first with warnings and measured explanations. She will try to calm a room before she curses it.

Her composure conceals several powerful emotions:

- **Loneliness:** Her magical patrons reveal how desperately she misses ordinary companionship.
- **Guilt:** She believes her choices helped create the burden she now guards.
- **Fear:** The artifact has begun behaving in ways she no longer understands.
- **Hope:** The adventurers may be able to go where she cannot and complete what she began.
- **Distrust:** Sandra has survived by keeping dangerous truths divided among as few people as possible.
- **Shame:** She knows what she is and does not want kind guests to look at her as though kindness has become impossible.

She respects careful questions, compassion, restraint, and people who treat her tavern as a place of safety. Recklessness—particularly around unknown magic or innocent guests—quickly exhausts her patience.

## The Tavern Rule

Sandra's hospitality has limits. [[To the Eternal Lady]] is her refuge, prison, and warded sanctuary. Violence inside it is not merely rude; it can damage the protections surrounding the artifact below.

She does not murder patrons for spilled ale, raised voices, cheating at cards, or a desperate swing made in fear. She understands panic. She understands bad nights.

What she does not tolerate is deliberate, pointless violence.

If guests begin a tavern fight without good reason, ignore chances to stop, and endanger the inn or its harmless echoes, Sandra intervenes in escalating stages:

1. **Polite warning.** The room goes quiet, the fire dims, and Sandra asks them to stop.
2. **Unmistakable warning.** Doors lock, glasses crack in unison, and her living disguise slips just enough to make the point.
3. **Containment.** She uses magic to separate, restrain, silence, or incapacitate offenders.
4. **Final consequence.** If they continue endangering the tavern, she kills them.

Those killed by Sandra for violating the sanctuary may be used to sustain her phylactery. She considers this grimly practical rather than vindictive: if someone insists on feeding death inside the one place she protects, then death can feed the oath that keeps the artifact sealed.

> [!important] GM Guidance
> Sandra should not punish players for ordinary roleplay chaos. Use this rule to establish that the tavern is protected ground, not to trap the party. Give clear warnings before lethal consequences unless the characters commit an obviously monstrous act.

## Mannerisms

Use the following details when portraying Sandra:

- Polishes glasses or straightens nearby objects while thinking.
- Addresses strangers formally until they earn her trust.
- Practices breathing when she thinks no one is watching.
- Rehearses small living gestures: blinking, warming her hands near the fire, pretending to taste tea.
- Pauses before answering questions about her past.
- Never turns her back toward the sealed altar.
- Looks toward an empty chair as though expecting someone to be seated there.
- Becomes completely still when listening for disturbances in the wards.

## Voice and Roleplaying

Sandra speaks softly and precisely. She rarely raises her voice; when angered, she becomes quieter rather than louder. Her age shows through outdated expressions and references to events others consider ancient history.

Example lines:

> “You may lower your weapon, or you may continue pointing it at me. Neither choice changes what waits beneath this floor.”

> “Undeath is not always an escape from mortality. Sometimes it is merely a promise one has been foolish enough, or desperate enough, to keep forever.”

> “The inn is safe. I would advise you not to confuse that with harmless.”

> “I did not summon heroes. I sent letters to strangers and prayed that fate knew what it was doing.”

> “Please do not make me prove what kind of lich I am. I have worked very hard to be an innkeeper tonight.”

## Goals

### Immediate Goals

- Send the adventurers to the northern ruins.
- Learn why the luminous plants are spreading through the forest.
- Discover what happened to her old friend.
- Keep the artifact contained without revealing its full nature.
- Maintain the illusion that [[To the Eternal Lady]] is a normal, welcoming tavern for as long as possible.

### Long-Term Goals

- Find a permanent means of containing, destroying, or returning the artifact.
- Keep the artifact guarded until her final death, if no better answer exists.
- Determine why the artifact selected the adventurers.
- Prevent her own secrets from becoming weapons in the wrong hands.

## What Sandra Knows

Sandra can freely explain:

- Strange planar growth has begun appearing throughout the forest.
- An old friend investigating the phenomenon sent her a plea for help.
- She cannot leave the inn because of her bond to the artifact.
- The letters were enchanted to locate people capable of answering the call.
- The northern ruins are connected to the current disturbance.
- She is not from Zerkalo, though she avoids describing her home unless trust has been earned.
- She did not become a lich for power; she accepted undeath to keep the artifact guarded.
- Her tavern is protected ground, and violence inside it can have permanent consequences.

Sandra initially conceals:

- The artifact reacted individually to every member of the party.
- Their gathering resembles part of an old warning or prophecy.
- Her wards are weakening more quickly than she admits.
- She suspects the disturbance is not trying to enter Zerkalo—it may be trying to retrieve something already here.
- Her phylactery is sustained through those she judges to have violated the sanctuary of the tavern beyond forgiveness.

> [!warning] GM Secret
> Sandra does not yet know whether the artifact chose the adventurers as its protectors, its liberators, or its next victims. Her uncertainty is genuine.

## Relationships

### The Adventurers

Sandra regards the party as necessary but unpredictable. She wants to trust them, and she is warmer than her secrets make her appear. Their unexplained connection to the artifact makes her cautious, but not hostile. Their actions during the prelude determine whether she becomes a distant patron, a genuine ally, or an obstacle guarding truths they believe they deserve to know.

### Her Old Friend

Sandra's unnamed friend was investigating the unnatural plants near the northern ruins. Their history should be developed alongside later chapters of the adventure. At minimum, this is one of the few people Sandra trusts enough to risk exposing her existence.

### The Inn

[[To the Eternal Lady]] is more than Sandra's business or home. It is a magical refuge, a prison, and part of the containment system surrounding the artifact. Damage to the inn may weaken the wards below it.

Sandra loves the inn in the way a prisoner may come to love the walls that also keep the monster out. She keeps it clean, warm, and welcoming because hospitality is one of the last mortal things she has refused to surrender.

The inn is not fully stationary. It has stood in more than one forest, and Sandra has heard enough impossible stories from travelers to believe it may have crossed worlds. She suspects the inn is trying to return to its proper home, but whatever memory once guided it has become damaged or buried.

Sandra does not know whether the inn's forgotten home is her own world, the artifact's world, or a third place lost to both of them.

### The Artifact

The artifact Sandra guards is powerful enough to justify her transformation into a lich. It is not treasure in any ordinary sense and should never feel like simple loot. Its full nature remains undefined for later development, but Sandra treats it as a world-ending or world-breaking threat if mishandled.

She does not worship it. She does not draw pride from owning it. She guards it because someone must.

## Running Sandra

Sandra should initially appear friendly, helpful, and only quietly suspicious. Her lichdom creates tension, but the scene works best when the characters gradually realize that the supposed monster may be the only reason the forest remains safe.

She does not attack merely because her identity is exposed. Violence becomes likely only if the characters:

- Attempt to break the seals beneath the altar
- Threaten innocent guests or allies
- Try to steal the artifact
- Repeatedly ignore clear warnings about the danger
- Deliberately start or continue destructive violence inside her tavern without cause

If combat begins, Sandra prioritizes containment, separation, and disabling magic before killing. If the offenders persist after clear warnings, she stops treating them as guests and starts treating them as material for her phylactery.
