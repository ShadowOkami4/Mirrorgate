# Chapter 3: The Sanctum of the Flower in the Mirror

> [!info] Chapter Information
> **Adventure:** [[The Pact in the Twilight]] (SA-01)  
> **Level:** 5  
> **Summary:** The second half of the dungeon, inside [[The Mirrorgate]]. The party explores the [[Sanctum of the Flower in the Mirror]], sees the floating shards of many worlds from its outer ring, solves its puzzles, and reaches the great hall, where they find [[Maria Rose]]—and where the red fog rises.

## Overview

The sanctum is the half of the Shrine of the Unreachable Bloom that the rift swallowed. It is not a reflection, and it does not behave like the rest of the Mirrorgate. Most of it is perfectly preserved, as if frozen in time; at its edges, the Mirrorgate is slowly bleeding through.

## 3.1 — Through the Glass

The party steps into the great mirror in the temple's worship hall and out of a matching mirror in the sanctum.

> [!quote] Read Aloud
> There is no cold, no falling, no sense of passing through anything at all. One step, and the ruin is gone.
>
> You stand in a hall of white stone, swept clean. Candles burn in their holders without dripping. A carved flower opens above the doorway ahead, and beneath it, words run the right way round.
>
> Behind you stands the mirror you came through. In it, the worship hall of the ruined temple waits, broken and grey, exactly as you left it.

The inscription above the doorway reads *the flower in the mirror*: the other half of the saying from the temple's lintel. A character who has seen the temple's lintel recognizes it at once.

- **DC 13 Intelligence (Arcana):** This is not a reflection. In the Mirrorgate, reflected writing reads backward. This writing does not.
- The mirror back to the temple works in both directions. The party can retreat to rest in the temple if they need to.

## 3.2 — The Frozen Halls

The inner sanctum is perfectly preserved, as though time stopped on the night of the ritual.

> [!quote] Read Aloud
> Hangings in silver and blue hang straight and unfaded. Offerings lie on low altars: bread that has not gone stale, flowers that have not wilted, cups of wine without a speck of dust. A broom leans against a wall where someone set it down, meaning to come back.
>
> Nothing here has aged a single day.

**Clues.** More of the shrine's diaries and handbook entries can be found here, written in the hours before the ritual. *(Contents not yet written; see the side quest in the [[Temple of the Moon in Water#Side Quest: The Disconnected Shrine|temple's entry]].)*

### Required Puzzle: The Reflected Bloom

The way out of the frozen halls is a door carved with a large flower whose eight stone petals are all folded shut. Each petal can be turned to point outward or inward. Beside the door stands a tall mirror. In the mirror, the carved flower is shown fully open—but some of its petals point in strange directions.

**Solution.** The door opens when the petals are set to match the flower in the mirror, *reversed* as a reflection would be: a petal shown on the left in the mirror must be set on the right of the real door. Setting the petals to copy the mirror exactly does not work.

**Hints:**

- **DC 12 Intelligence (Investigation):** The flower in the mirror is not the door's reflection; it shows the door as it should be.
- **DC 13 Wisdom (Insight):** Everything in a mirror is reversed. So is the answer.

### Optional Puzzle: The Frozen Offering

On the main altar, a bowl of offerings is frozen mid-moment. A coin hangs in the air above it, caught as it fell. Nothing on the altar can be moved or touched; hands slide off as if from glass. Three small silver bells hang beside the altar, each engraved with a moon phase: full, half, and new.

**Solution.** Ringing the bells in the order the shrine honoured the waning moon, **full, half, new**, releases the moment. The coin drops into the bowl with a clear ring, and the offerings can be touched again.

**Hints:**

- **DC 12 Intelligence (Investigation):** The bells are worn unevenly; the full-moon bell most of all.
- Parties who solved the Phase Lock in Chapter 2 have already learned that the shrine honoured the moon from full to dark.

**Reward.** Beneath the offerings lie a **Pearl of Power** and a **Potion of Greater Healing**.

## 3.3 — The Bleeding Edge

Beyond the frozen halls, the sanctum begins to break. Here, the Mirrorgate is bleeding through.

> [!quote] Read Aloud
> The white stone is split by roots that shine like polished silver. Moss glows faintly blue along every crack. Pale, glowing plants drift in the air on long trailing roots, like jellyfish made of leaves.
>
> And then, in the middle of a corridor, it stops. The roots end in a straight line across the floor, as if they had grown against a wall that is not there. On the far side, the stone is untouched.

The plants are Mirrorgate flora: silverbark roots, seam moss, and lanternweed (see [[Life in the Mirrorgate]]). They break the sanctum apart only up to a certain point. Beyond it, something holds them back.

- **DC 14 Intelligence (Arcana):** A power is holding the line, but it is unlike any ward the character knows. Its source cannot be found.

> [!question] Open Mystery
> What power holds the Mirrorgate back from the rest of the sanctum?

Where the Mirrorgate breaks through, shardkin gather.

> [!danger] Encounter: The Bleeding Edge
> - 2 [[Counterfeit|Counterfeits]]
> - 2 [[Afterimage|Afterimages]]
> - 4 [[Glint|Glints]]
>
> **Difficulty:** 2,200 XP, a Low encounter for four 5th-level characters.  
> **Scaling:** For three characters, use one Counterfeit. For five characters, add one Afterimage and two Glints.

The counterfeits step out of the polished silverbark and take the faces of two characters. Let them sow confusion; see [[Counterfeit]].

## 3.4 — The Outer Ring

At its edge, the sanctum opens onto a broken walkway that runs around its outer wall. Beyond it lies the Mirrorgate itself.

> [!quote] Read Aloud
> The walkway ends at nothing. Beyond it, pale mist stretches in every direction, and in that mist hang islands: pieces of land, turning slowly, each with its own sense of up and down.
>
> On one, you see a market square you almost recognize. On another, a village beside a river. A waterfall pours upward from the edge of a third. Above them all, the sky is a cracked mirror, every piece showing a different sky.

The floating shards are reflections of real places: in [[Kagameru]], and in other worlds. The sanctum has no path to any of them. It is disconnected from the rest of the Mirrorgate, and the party can only look.

### Shards of Home

Most shards are reflected from water: rivers, lakes, the sea. Characters from **Kagameru** may spot places they know.

Characters from **other worlds** may spot something they never expected to see again: a landmark from their homeland that stands near a river, a lake, or the ocean.

> [!tip] Running Shards of Home
> Ask each player whose character comes from another world to describe a place from their homeland that stands near water. Then describe it to them, hanging in the mist—reversed, as every reflection is. It is a powerful moment, and it shows them that the Mirrorgate touches their world too.

### Strange Sights

Not everything in the Mirrorgate makes sense. Roll on the table or choose.

| 1d6 | Sight |
| ---: | --- |
| 1 | A lighthouse on a lonely shard whose beam shines darkness instead of light |
| 2 | A city street in which it is raining upward |
| 3 | A sky-fragment with two suns, one of them setting while the other rises |
| 4 | A shard of open sea with no shore, its waves rolling over nothing |
| 5 | A crowded market where every person stands perfectly still, mid-step |
| 6 | The party's own campsite from last night, the fire still burning |

### The Fauna of the Outer Ring

Where the Mirrorgate has broken through, its wildlife has followed: silverhorns graze on the silver roots, twinwings circle the walkway in perfect pairs, and seam beetles glow in the cracks (see [[Life in the Mirrorgate]]). They are wary but not hostile. This is the fauna Maria has been studying.

### Optional Puzzle: The Echo Fox

An **echo fox** watches the party from the walkway. It mimics sounds it has heard—and one of them is a woman's voice.

> [!quote] Read Aloud
> The fox tilts its head. Then it opens its mouth, and a woman's voice comes out, warm and tired: *"There you are. No, don't run. I won't hurt you. I only want to see where you sleep."*

The voice is Maria's. The fox has heard her many times while she studied it.

**Solution.** If the party treats the fox gently—offering food, speaking softly, not grabbing for it—it leads them along the walkway to its den. A character who repeats Maria's words back to it in a calm voice earns its trust at once.

**Hints:**

- **DC 12 Wisdom (Animal Handling):** The fox is curious, not afraid. It wants to be followed.
- **DC 13 Wisdom (Insight):** The fox repeats this phrase more than any other. It has heard it often, from someone it trusts.

**Reward.** In the fox's den lies **Maria's satchel**, left behind on one of her studies: her field notes on the fauna of the outer ring, a **Potion of Healing**, and a small silver hand mirror. The notes are a clue to what Maria found. *(Contents not yet written.)*

## 3.5 — The Procession Hall

A long hall leads toward the heart of the sanctum. Here, the night of the ritual is still happening.

> [!quote] Read Aloud
> Figures of pale light walk the length of the hall in a slow procession: priests in long robes, carrying candles that do not flicker. They walk, and walk, and never arrive. At the far end, a great door stands closed.
>
> Among them, there are gaps: empty places in the procession where someone should be walking.

The figures are [[Afterimage|afterimages]]: moments left behind from the night of the ritual. They replay the procession endlessly. They are not the priests themselves.

### Required Puzzle: The Unfinished Procession

The procession has empty places, and the door at the end will not open until the procession is complete.

**Solution.** The party must step into the empty places and walk with the procession to the door, at its pace, facing north. When every gap is filled and the procession reaches the door, it opens.

**Hints:**

- **DC 12 Wisdom (Perception):** The gaps in the procession are always in the same places, as if waiting for someone.
- **DC 13 Intelligence (Religion):** Processions in the handbook describe the faithful walking together toward the north. Nobody walks alone.

**Breaking the procession.** If a character attacks an afterimage, steps out of place, or turns their back on the north during the walk, the moment shatters, and the afterimages turn on the party.

> [!danger] Encounter: The Night of the Ritual
> Only if the procession is broken.
> - 8 [[Afterimage|Afterimages]]
> - 4 [[Glint|Glints]]
>
> **Difficulty:** 2,000 XP, a Low encounter for four 5th-level characters.  
> **Scaling:** For three characters, use six Afterimages and no Glints. For five characters, add two Afterimages.

After the fight, the door can be opened by force or patience: the procession reforms after 10 minutes and can be tried again.

## 3.6 — The Moonless Pool

Before the great hall lies a round chamber with a dry pool in its floor and a round opening in its ceiling. Through the opening, only the cracked sky of the Mirrorgate can be seen. There is no moon.

> [!quote] Read Aloud
> A carved moon fills the great door beyond the pool, and above it, the same words you have seen before: *the moon in the water.* But the pool is dry, and no moon shines down through the opening above.

### Required Puzzle: The Moonless Pool

**Solution.** The door opens when "the moon is in the water": the pool must hold water, and a moon must shine in it. Water can be brought from the outer ring's springs or poured from waterskins. The "moon" can be any round light held above the pool so that its reflection lies at the pool's centre: a lantern, a *Light* spell, or the **Driftglobe** from Chapter 2.

**Hints:**

- **DC 12 Intelligence (Investigation):** A shallow channel runs from the pool toward the outer ring, long dry.
- **DC 13 Intelligence (Religion):** The faithful said that wherever the moon could be seen in water, the god could be found.

## 3.7 — The Great Hall

The great door opens onto the **great hall** of the sanctum: the place where the ritual to revive the moon god was performed.

> [!quote] Read Aloud
> The hall is enormous and round, and every part of it faces a great empty dais in the north. Scorch marks spread across the floor in a perfect circle, as if something burned here once and was frozen in the act.
>
> In the middle of the circle, a woman turns to look at you. She is pale, translucent, and very tired, and she smiles as if she has been waiting a long time.
>
> "Sandra sent you," she says. "Of course she did."

This is [[Maria Rose]].

### Meeting Maria

Maria tells the party what happened to the shrine: the priests' ritual to revive their moon god, its failure, and the rift that tore the sanctum away. She also warns them that a **strange, evil power looms in the shrine**.

**If the party found the clues**, Maria's account confirms and completes what they pieced together from the diaries and handbook entries.

**If the party missed the clues**, they are surprised and ask what she means by "the shrine." Maria explains its history in full. Missing the clues does not lock the party out of the story; they simply hear it from her.

**Her research.** Maria then shares what she found while exploring the sanctum and studying the wild fauna of its outer ring. If the party brings her satchel from the echo fox's den, she is delighted, and her account is more complete. *(The details of her findings are not yet written.)*

If the party mentions Sandra's message—*the kettle is still on*—Maria laughs for the first time in a long while.

### The Red Fog

While they talk, the red fog rises.

1. An extremely strange **red fog** begins to seep into the hall.
2. A **voice** speaks from the fog. It speaks to the *players*, not only to their characters. It calls them **"souls of the guided, who control many bodies."**
3. The voice delivers a monologue: it hates the **plague** of those like them, and it will **unmake** every one of them.
4. The fog closes around Maria. She screams. Then the scream turns into a giggle. She has been **corrupted**.
5. The battle begins.

> [!quote] Read Aloud (draft wording)
> The fog is red, the colour of a wound, and it moves against the air instead of with it. When the voice comes, it does not come from the fog. It comes from somewhere just behind your eyes.
>
> *"Souls of the guided. You who control many bodies. I know what you are."*
>
> *"You are a plague. You and all those like you. And I will unmake every one of you."*

> [!danger] Encounter: Maria, Corrupted
> A stat block for the corrupted Maria is not yet written.

> [!note] Open Questions

> - Why does it know that the characters are guided by players?
> - Is the corrupted Maria the boss of SA-01, whose fight uses *Resurrections* — part: **Badeline Chase**?
