# Chapter 2: The Temple of the Moon in Water

> [!info] Chapter Information
> **Adventure:** [[The Pact in the Twilight]] (SA-01)  
> **Level:** 4  
> **Summary:** A puzzle and discovery dungeon. The party explores the ruined [[Temple of the Moon in Water]], fights [[Shardkin]], solves the puzzles of the shrine's builders, and reaches the worship hall with its great mirror.

## Overview

The temple is the surviving half of the **Shrine of the Unreachable Bloom**. Its outer rooms are badly broken down; the deeper the party goes, the better preserved the temple becomes, and the more it reveals about the faith of its builders.

## 2.1 — The Entrance Hall

What is left of the entrance hall is barely a room. Half the roof has fallen in, and young trees grow through the floor.

> [!quote] Read Aloud
> Beyond the cracked lintel, the entrance hall lies open to the sky. Fallen stones the size of carts lie where they dropped. Rainwater stands in the hollows of the floor, and ferns grow from every crack.
>
> On the far side, between two leaning pillars, a passage leads deeper into the temple.

- **DC 12 Wisdom (Perception):** On one wall, a carving survives under the moss: a crowd of figures, all facing the same direction, looking up at a moon.
- **DC 13 Intelligence (Investigation):** One wall ends in a clean, straight edge, as if cut with a blade. Beyond it is open forest. This is part of the shrine that is now in the Mirrorgate.
- **DC 14 Intelligence (Religion):** The carvings show worship of a moon. No temple of a moon god is known in any living faith of [[Kagameru]].

Drifting shards (see [[SA-01 Chapter 1#Drifting Shards|Chapter 1]]) turn slowly in the air here, more of them than the party saw in the forest.

### Optional Puzzle: The Half Mosaic

Beside the cut-away wall, a floor mosaic survives—but only half of it. The other half was on the part of the shrine that is now gone. What remains shows half of a round shape and half of a flower, ending in the same clean, straight line as the wall.

**Solution.** The image was symmetrical. A character who holds a mirror or polished surface upright along the cut edge sees the complete mosaic in the reflection: a full moon above an open flower. When the full image appears, a tile at the centre of the moon sinks with a soft click, revealing a small hidden compartment.

**Hints:**

- **DC 12 Wisdom (Perception):** The surviving half is perfectly symmetrical along the cut edge.
- **DC 13 Intelligence (Investigation):** The central tile is set slightly lower than the others, as if it were meant to be pressed by something other than a hand.

**Reward.** Inside the compartment lies a **Driftglobe**, a small glass sphere that glows like the moon.

## 2.2 — The Courtyard Pool

At the heart of the outer temple lies an open courtyard with a still, dark pool.

> [!quote] Read Aloud
> The pool is perfectly round and perfectly still. Not a leaf floats on it. Worn stone benches surround it, all set on its southern side, so that whoever sat on them would look across the water toward the north.

The shrine's faithful gathered here at night to watch the moon rise over the pool.

- **DC 13 Intelligence (History or Religion):** Every bench, carving, and altar so far faces north. Whatever was worshipped here was always looked for in the northern sky.

The pool is also the easiest source of water for the Moon Door (area 2.4).

### Optional Puzzle: The Sunken Offering

The pool is deep and black; nothing can be seen beneath its surface. The faithful once left offerings here, in a place they could find only through reflection.

**Solution.** A character who holds a light above the northernmost bench sees its reflection land on one exact spot of the water. Directly beneath that spot, an arm's length down, a small stone box rests on a ledge. Anyone reaching anywhere else finds only cold water and smooth stone.

**Hints:**

- **DC 12 Intelligence (Investigation):** The northernmost bench is worn deeper than the others; it was where the priest sat to watch the moon rise.
- **DC 14 Intelligence (Religion):** Old shrines to moon deities often hid offerings where the moon's reflection would fall.

**Reward.** The stone box holds a **Potion of Greater Healing** and **30 gp** in old coins stamped with a crescent moon.

## 2.3 — The Fallen Corridors

Beyond the courtyard, the temple becomes a maze of broken corridors. Many have collapsed completely and cannot be cleared.

### Required Puzzle: The Crescent Path

Small crescent moons are carved at the corners of the corridors, half-hidden under moss. At every junction, only one passage leads on; the others end in rubble after a few dozen feet.

**Solution.** The faithful always walked toward the moon, and the moon was always in the north. At each junction, the open passage is the one where the carved crescent points north.

**Hints:**

- **DC 12 Wisdom (Perception):** Each crescent's tips point in a different direction.
- **DC 13 Wisdom (Survival):** Every crescent that marks an open passage points north.

**Wrong turns.** Each wrong turn costs about 10 minutes. On the second wrong turn, loose stones fall from the ceiling. *Dexterity Saving Throw:* DC 12, each creature in the passage. *Failure:* 7 (2d6) Bludgeoning damage. *Success:* Half damage.

> [!tip] The Lady's Compass
> Sandra's compass points to the inn, not north, so it cannot solve this puzzle directly. A clever party may still use it: the inn lies south of the temple, so north is the opposite way.

### The Husks

The last stretch of the Crescent Path leads the party past something waiting in the dark.

> [!quote] Read Aloud
> Something shifts among the fallen stones. A figure drags itself upright: a body, grey-skinned and hollow-eyed, with plates of glass pushing out through its skin like frost through a windowpane. Then another. Then another.

These are [[Glass Husk|glass husks]]. They look like zombies, but they are not. A character who succeeds on a **DC 13 Intelligence (Arcana or Religion) check** realizes that no necromancy animates them: something like glass moves where their souls should be.

> [!danger] Encounter: The Fallen Corridors
> - 5 [[Glass Husk|Glass Husks]]
>
> **Difficulty:** 500 XP, a Low encounter for four 4th-level characters.  
> **Scaling:** For three characters, use four husks. For five characters, use seven.

Remember the husks' Burst of Glass trait: crowding around them is dangerous.

> [!question] Open Mystery
> Glass husks almost never appear in the material world. Who these husks were, and why they are here, is part of the shrine's mystery.

## 2.4 — The Moon Door

**Required puzzle.** The way deeper is barred by a heavy stone door carved with a full moon above a wide, dry stone basin.

> [!quote] Read Aloud
> The door has no handle, no keyhole, and no hinges you can see. Carved into its centre is a full moon, wide as a cartwheel. At its foot lies a shallow stone basin, dry and full of dust.

**Solution.** The door opens when the carved moon is reflected in water. Filling the basin with water from the courtyard pool, a rain puddle, or a waterskin does it; so does holding a polished surface, such as a shield or mirror, so that it reflects the carving. When the moon "appears in the water," the door slides aside.

**Hints:**

- **DC 12 Intelligence (Investigation):** The basin is placed so that, if it held water, the moon carving would be reflected in it.
- **DC 13 Intelligence (Religion):** The faithful looked for their god "in the water," as the lintel says.

Forcing the door is possible but slow: it has AC 17, 60 Hit Points, and Immunity to Poison and Psychic damage.

## 2.5 — The Hall of Backward Words

Beyond the Moon Door lies a long hall lined with shelves, most of them collapsed. This was where the shrine's priests kept their books.

> [!quote] Read Aloud
> Rotten shelves lean against the walls, spilling the remains of books that crumble at a touch. At the far end, a door is covered in carved letters that make no sense at all—until you notice that every one of them is backward.

**Clue: Handbook Entries.** Among the ruined books, a few pages of a priests' handbook survive. They describe the shrine's rites: how the faithful faced north toward the moon, and how the god's reflection was believed to live behind the glass. *(Contents not yet written.)*

**Required Puzzle: Backward Words.** The door is inscribed with a prayer that reads correctly only in a mirror. It opens when the prayer is spoken aloud.

**Solution.** A character reads the inscription in any reflective surface—a mirror, a polished blade, a pool of water poured on the floor—and speaks the prayer.

**Hints:**

- **DC 12 Intelligence (Investigation):** The letters are not a foreign script; they are ordinary letters, reversed.
- **DC 14 Intelligence (Religion)**, after reading the handbook pages: This is a known prayer of the shrine, meant to be spoken aloud.

### Optional Puzzle: The Star Ceiling

The ceiling of the hall is painted with a night sky, faded but still visible, its stars picked out in tiny silver studs. Eight stone moons, each showing a different phase, lie scattered among the fallen shelves; each has a peg on its back. Eight empty sockets form a ring in the northern part of the painted sky.

**Solution.** The moons belong in the sockets in the order of the moon's phases, beginning with the full moon in the northernmost socket and continuing around the ring: full, waning gibbous, last quarter, waning crescent, new, waxing crescent, first quarter, waxing gibbous. When the last moon is placed, a panel in the wall beneath the painted sky swings open.

**Hints:**

- **DC 12 Intelligence (Investigation):** One socket, in the far north of the ring, is larger than the others and ringed with silver.
- **DC 13 Intelligence (Nature or Religion):** The eight stones show the eight phases of the moon, which follow one another in a fixed order.

**Reward.** Behind the panel lie a **Cloak of Protection**, folded and perfectly preserved, and a **Spell Scroll** of *Moonbeam*.

## 2.6 — The Seen Door

The next door is plain, unmarked, and impossible to open while anyone is watching.

> [!quote] Read Aloud
> The door is unremarkable: smooth stone, a simple ring for a handle. When you pull it, it does not move. When you look away for a moment, you hear stone shift. When you look back, it is shut again.

**Required Puzzle: The Seen Door.** The door cannot be opened while any creature looks at it directly. It can be opened by a creature that watches it only in a reflection.

**Solution.** A character turns their back to the door and opens it while watching it in a mirror, a polished shield, or water. Alternatively, every character looks away while one opens it blind.

**Hints:**

- **DC 13 Wisdom (Perception):** The door only moves when nobody is looking at it.
- **DC 13 Intelligence (Religion)**, after the handbook pages: The faithful were taught to look at holy things in reflection, never directly.

**Clue: The Priest's Diary.** Beyond the door lies a small cell where a priest once lived. On a stone desk lies a diary, its last entries written in the days before a great ritual. *(Contents not yet written.)*

## 2.7 — The Antechamber

The final room before the worship hall is better preserved than anything the party has seen so far. Here, the shrine's last guardians wait.

> [!danger] Encounter: The Antechamber
> - 1 [[Remnant]]
> - 2 [[Glass Husk|Glass Husks]]
> - 4 [[Glint|Glints]]
>
> **Difficulty:** 1,050 XP, a Low encounter for four 4th-level characters.  
> **Scaling:** For three characters, remove one Glass Husk and two Glints. For five characters, add one Glass Husk and two Glints.

The remnant speaks in broken fragments, as remnants do. What it says, and who it was, is not yet written.

### Required Puzzle: The Phase Lock

The great doors of the worship hall are sealed. Set into them is a wheel with the eight moon phases around its rim and a single pointer that can be turned. Above the doors, a line from the shrine's prayers is carved: *"We greet her full, and we let her go dark."*

**Solution.** The doors open when the pointer is turned through the phases in order, starting at the **full moon** and ending at the **new moon**: full, waning gibbous, last quarter, waning crescent, new. Turning it the wrong way resets the wheel with a heavy clunk.

**Hints:**

- **DC 12 Intelligence (Investigation):** The pointer moves in both directions, but only one direction makes the wheel click.
- **DC 13 Intelligence (Religion):** The carved line describes the waning moon: from full to dark.

Parties who solved the Star Ceiling have already handled the phases in order and may recognize the pattern.

## 2.8 — The Worship Hall

The great doors open onto the largest room in the temple: the shrine's **worship hall**, where the faithful gathered to pray to the moon.

> [!quote] Read Aloud
> The hall is vast. Rows of kneeling stones fill the floor, every one of them facing north. High in the northern wall, a great round opening looks out at the sky.
>
> Beneath it stands a mirror taller than any door in the temple, its frame carved with a moon and a flower.
>
> The mirror shows you exactly as you are. But the hall behind you is wrong. In the glass, there are no fallen stones and no roots in the floor. The pillars stand whole, the walls rise higher than they should, and the doorways open onto rooms you did not pass on your way in.
>
> You turn around. Behind you, the hall is still a ruin.

### Why the Mirror Is Here

In the faith of the moon god, everyone who prayed—the priests included—had to look toward the moon. From this temple, the moon was always in the north. So the priest stood at the **back** of the hall, behind the crowd, and the faithful faced away from them, toward the northern sky.

The great mirror solved a simple problem. A newcomer who needed to watch the priest to know when to kneel, rise, or speak, or anyone who needed to see the priest for any other reason, could look into the mirror instead of turning around. No one ever had to turn their back on the moon.

- **DC 12 Wisdom (Insight)** or **DC 13 Intelligence (Religion):** The raised dais at the southern end of the hall, behind the kneeling stones, is where the priest stood. From the kneeling stones, the dais is visible only in the mirror.

### The Mirror's Power

The great mirror holds the power of the rift that tore the shrine apart. A creature that uses *Detect Magic* or a similar feature near it is overwhelmed, and may be blinded. See [[Temple of the Moon in Water#The Mirror's Power|The Mirror's Power]].

### Entering the Mirror

A creature that steps into the great mirror passes through it and arrives in the [[Sanctum of the Flower in the Mirror]]. This is the subject of Chapter 3.

## Rewards and Consequences

- **Experience.** If you use milestone levelling, the party reaches **5th level** at the end of this chapter.
- **Treasure.** In the priest's cell (area 2.6): **40 gp** in old coins stamped with a crescent moon, a **Potion of Healing**, and a silver holy symbol of the moon god worth **25 gp**.
- **Optional puzzles.** A **Driftglobe** (the Half Mosaic); a **Potion of Greater Healing** and **30 gp** (the Sunken Offering); a **Cloak of Protection** and a **Spell Scroll** of *Moonbeam* (the Star Ceiling).
- **Clues.** Note which clues the party found: the handbook entries and the priest's diary.
