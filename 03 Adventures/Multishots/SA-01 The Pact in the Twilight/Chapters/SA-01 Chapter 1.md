# Chapter 1: What the Thieves Found

> [!warning] To Be Rewritten
> This first draft does not follow the intended direction of the story and will be rewritten. Its scene 1.4, *The Pack*, was based on a misreading of the title: the adventure is about a **pact** (German *Pakt*), not a pack of wolves.

> [!info] Chapter Information
> **Adventure:** [[The Pact in the Twilight]] (SA-01)  
> **Level:** 4  
> **Summary:** The party leaves [[To the Eternal Lady]] and travels north through the [[Forest of Thieves]]. They learn what became of the missing thieves, meet the pack at dusk, and reach the [[Temple of the Moon in Water]] at twilight on the second day.

## Overview

The journey north takes about a day and a half on foot. The forest grows stranger the farther the party goes: the luminous plants spread, a stream forgets which way it flows, and creatures from behind the glass wander between the trees.

| Scene | When | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1.1 Leaving the Lady | first morning | Sandra's parting gift and last warnings |
| 1.2 The Ford That Runs Twice | first morning | a stream slips in time; a silverhorn drinks |
| 1.3 The Thieves' Last Camp | first afternoon | the fate of the missing thieves; combat with shardkin |
| 1.4 The Pack | first dusk | the pack circles the party's camp |
| 1.5 The Temple at Twilight | second dusk | arrival at the Temple of the Moon in Water |

> [!note] Distances
> The temple lies about 30 km north of the inn, a day and a half of travel through dense forest with no road. One stream crossing lies a short walk north of the inn. If the map of the region changes, keep the timing: one night in the forest, arrival at twilight.

## 1.1 — Leaving the Lady

The party leaves at first light. The common room is quiet at this hour; the echo-guests have not yet "arrived," and the fire burns without crackling.

> [!quote] Read Aloud
> Sandra is waiting by the door with a lantern in her hands. In daylight, her disguise is flawless again: warm skin, a tired smile, a shawl pulled around shoulders that cannot feel the cold.
>
> "The map will show you the safest path," she says. "It may change its mind on the way. Trust it more than you trust the forest, and less than you trust each other."
>
> She holds out the lantern. Its small flame leans sideways, toward her, as if the inn were pulling at it.
>
> "So you can find your way back. I may not be standing where you left me."

**The Lady's Lantern.** The lantern's flame always leans toward To the Eternal Lady, wherever the inn currently stands. It sheds light like an ordinary lantern and never needs oil. Sandra wants it returned.

If asked, Sandra adds the following:

- Maria Rose wrote that she was "close to the heart of it" and that the temple's pool "showed the wrong sky."
- The thieves who disappeared were last seen heading north. Sandra suspects they went to loot the ruin.
- "If you find Maria, tell her the kettle is still on." Sandra refuses to explain. It is an old joke between friends.

## 1.2 — The Ford That Runs Twice

A short walk north of the inn, the path meets a cold, clear stream. Luminous plants crowd its banks, more of them than the party saw in the Prelude.

> [!quote] Read Aloud
> The stream is shallow and quick, chattering over smooth stones. Then, for the space of a breath, it falls silent—and runs backward. Leaves drift upstream. A ripple folds back into the stone that made it.
>
> A heartbeat later, the water rushes on as if nothing happened.

The stream slips backward for a moment every few minutes. Crossing is safe, but unsettling.

- **DC 12 Wisdom (Perception):** The plants along the banks pulse brighter just before each slip.
- **DC 13 Intelligence (Arcana):** This is a small temporal slip of the kind found near the [[Scars of the Primordial War]]. It is new; the stones show no wear from it.
- **DC 14 Wisdom (Survival):** Upstream, the slips grow stronger. Whatever causes them lies to the north.

### The Silverhorn

On the far bank, a deer stands drinking. Its antlers look like polished glass.

> [!quote] Read Aloud
> Its reflection appears in the water a moment before the deer lowers its head to drink. The animal looks up at you—and its reflection was already looking.

The creature is a **silverhorn**, a native of the Mirrorgate (see [[Life in the Mirrorgate]]). It is harmless and flees if approached. A character who succeeds on a **DC 15 Intelligence (Nature or Arcana) check** knows that no such animal is native to [[Kagameru]].

The silverhorn is a clue: things are coming *through*.

> [!gm] Working Design: The Luminous Plants
> The plants are Mirrorgate flora growing through hairline seams in the world, the way [[Life in the Mirrorgate|seam moss]] grows along the broken edges of shards. The closer the party comes to the temple, the wider the seams and the thicker the growth. This is a proposal and can be changed.

## 1.3 — The Thieves' Last Camp

In the afternoon, the party finds a camp beside a rock shelf where the luminous plants grow thick as a meadow.

> [!quote] Read Aloud
> A cold fire pit. Bedrolls kicked aside. A cooking pot on its side, its stew long since dried to a crust. Packs, rope, a crossbow left loaded.
>
> Whoever camped here left in a hurry—or did not leave at all.
>
> In the middle of the rock shelf, a crack runs through the stone, no wider than a hand. It is full of light. When you look into it, you do not see the bottom. You see a sky: cracked into a hundred pieces, each piece a different colour.

These are the missing thieves from the Prelude. They came north to loot the ruin, camped here, and found the crack. Their leader, **Mags Tallow**, looked into it at night—and the crack looked back.

### The Mirror Wound

The crack is a small mirror wound into [[The Mirrorgate]]. A creature that touches it is subjected to the **Mirror Wound** effect described in [[Scar Hazards]]. Looking into it is safe for a moment, but anyone who stares for a minute or more must succeed on a **DC 12 Wisdom saving throw** or fall into the same trance that undid Mags; an ally can shake them out of it as an action.

- **DC 13 Intelligence (Investigation):** Four bedrolls, but only one set of tracks leaving the camp. They head north, running.
- **DC 14 Wisdom (Insight)** while examining the camp: Whatever happened here happened fast, in the middle of the night, and without a fight.

### What Remains of Mags

When the party has looked around for a few minutes, the shardkin stir.

> [!quote] Read Aloud
> The light in the crack flickers. Something pale climbs out of the plants: a woman's body, glass pushing through its skin like frost through a window. Small cold lights drift up around it.
>
> Then a voice behind you, broken into pieces—"Don't—look—at—it—Oswin, *don't*—"
>
> A figure like a woman seen through shattered glass steps out of the shadow of the rocks.

Mags was shattered into three. Her body is a [[Glass Husk]], her soul has splintered into [[Glint|Glints]], and her self survives as a [[Remnant]]. An [[Afterimage]] near the crack endlessly replays the moment Mags leaned over it.

> [!danger] Encounter: The Shattered Thief
> - 1 [[Remnant]] (Mags Tallow)
> - 1 [[Glass Husk]] (Mags's body)
> - 4 [[Glint|Glints]] (Mags's soul)
> - 1 [[Afterimage]] (the moment it happened)
>
> **Difficulty:** 1,150 XP, between a Low and a Moderate encounter for four 4th-level characters.  
> **Scaling:** For three characters, remove the Afterimage and one Glint. For five characters, add one Afterimage and one Glint.

**Tactics.** The remnant opens with Mirror of Self against the strongest-looking character, then fights beside the husk while the glints swarm anyone who is wounded. The afterimage ignores the party and keeps replaying its moment unless someone steps into it. The remnant calls out in fragments of Mags's voice—a name, half a warning, a laugh from a happier night.

**Learning Her Name.** A character who listens to the remnant can piece together who it was. A character who speaks the name *Mags* to a glint makes it hesitate for a round (see [[Glint]]).

### Oswin

When the fight ends, a thin voice calls down from an oak at the edge of the clearing. **Oswin Pike**, the youngest of the thieves, has been hiding in its branches for two days. He is starving, terrified, and very glad to see anyone who is not made of glass.

Oswin tells the party what happened:

- The band came north to rob the old temple. Mags had heard there was silver in it.
- They camped by the glowing plants. Mags found the crack, said it showed "the sky upside down," and sat watching it after the others fell asleep.
- In the night, she screamed—and came apart. The others ran.
- **Dunny**, the band's lookout, ran north toward the temple. Oswin climbed the tree and has not dared to come down.

Oswin wants to go home. If the party is kind to him, he gives them what the band had saved: **14 gp, 31 sp, a Potion of Healing,** and a page torn from Mags's notebook with a sketch of the temple. In the sketch, the courtyard pool shows a waterfall falling *up*.

> [!gm] Oswin's Fate
> Oswin can return to the inn using the Lady's Lantern as a guide, if the party lends it to him, or travel with the party. Sandra will feed and shelter him either way. Dunny's fate is left for Chapter 2.

## 1.4 — The Pack

The party makes camp as the light fails. This is the scene the adventure is named for.

> [!quote] Read Aloud
> The sun sinks behind the trees, and the forest goes gold, then grey, then the deep blue that is neither day nor night.
>
> In that blue, the wolves arrive.
>
> There are three of them, maybe four; it is hard to tell where one ends and the dusk begins. They are made of the twilight itself, grey fur edged with the last of the light, eyes the colour of the sky behind them. They do not snarl. They walk in a slow circle around your camp, close enough to touch, and watch you.

The wolves circle the camp for several minutes. They are watching how the party behaves *together*: who stands in front of whom, who reaches for a weapon, who reaches for a friend.

- **DC 12 Wisdom (Animal Handling or Insight):** The wolves are not hunting. They are testing.
- **DC 15 Wisdom (Perception):** The wolves cast no shadows, and they flicker when they step out of the dusk-light into the firelight.

When full night falls, the wolves fade into the dark between the trees. Their tracks end where the light ended.

**If the party attacks**, the wolves fight back, but only until night falls. Use the **Dire Wolf** stat block from the SRD 5.2.1 for three wolves, with the following trait:

> **Twilight Step.** As a Bonus Action while in Dim Light, the wolf teleports up to 30 feet to an unoccupied space in Dim Light that it can see. When the area around it becomes Darkness or Bright Light, the wolf vanishes and can't return until the next dusk or dawn.

**If the party stands together**—shoulder to shoulder, without attacking—the largest wolf steps forward, looks at each character in turn, and bows its head before fading. The party will see it again.

> [!gm] Working Design: What Is the Pack?
> The pack exists only in twilight, the hour between day and night, as the Mirrorgate exists between worlds. Its true nature is undecided. Possible directions:
> 1. **Mirrorgate natives** that slipped through the widening seams, as the silverhorn did. They are testing the party to decide whether it is prey or another pack.
> 2. **Guardians of the temple**, bound to its two halves, perhaps answering to [[Maria Rose]].
> 3. **A reflection of the party itself**, connected to the artifact that chose them: a pack that appears because the strangers have become one.
>
> Whichever direction is chosen, the wolves should not be simple monsters. They are a question the adventure answers.

## 1.5 — The Temple at Twilight

The second day's travel is quieter and stranger. The plants grow so thickly that the party walks through a field of pale blue light even at noon.

**Sandra's Map.** Around midday, the route drawn on Sandra's map shifts, curving east around a stretch of forest it had previously crossed. A character who succeeds on a **DC 15 Intelligence (Arcana) check** realizes that the map is avoiding a place where the seams have grown too wide.

**Making Good Time.** Have the party make a **DC 13 group Wisdom (Survival) check**. On a success, they reach the temple with time to rest. On a failure, the plants and shifting route slow them, and they arrive tired, without time for a Short Rest before nightfall.

> [!quote] Read Aloud
> The trees open onto a hill crowned with broken stone. Columns lean against one another like tired old men. Moss covers the steps.
>
> Above the entrance, a cracked lintel holds a line of old carved letters. Beyond it, in the heart of the ruin, lies a pool as still and dark as a mirror.
>
> The sun is going down again. In the pool's reflection, a column of silver water falls upward into the sky—though above the real pool there is nothing but evening.
>
> And in the reflection, deep inside the temple, a lantern is moving.

The party has reached the [[Temple of the Moon in Water]]. The lintel reads *the moon in the water*; a character who succeeds on a **DC 14 Intelligence (History or Religion) check** recognizes the old saying and notices that its other half is missing (see the temple's entry).

Whoever carries the lantern in the reflection is the subject of Chapter 2.

> [!gm] Working Design: The Lantern
> The moving lantern can be Dunny the lookout, lost inside the reflected temple; [[Maria Rose]], still searching; or something wearing one of their faces, such as a [[Counterfeit]]. Chapter 2 decides.

## Rewards and Consequences

- **Experience.** If you use milestone levelling, this chapter does not end a level.
- **Treasure.** The thieves' savings (14 gp, 31 sp, a Potion of Healing) and the Lady's Lantern, on loan from Sandra.
- **Oswin Pike.** If the party saved Oswin and treated him kindly, Sandra learns of it. Later, she trusts the party with more of the truth.
- **The pack.** Note whether the party attacked the wolves or stood together. The pack remembers.
