# Chapter 1: The Road to the Temple

> [!info] Chapter Information
> **Adventure:** [[The Pact in the Twilight]] (SA-01)  
> **Level:** 4  
> **Summary:** The party leaves [[To the Eternal Lady]] and travels north through the [[Forest of Thieves]]. They pass an owlbear family, survive an attack by thieves, and finally reach the [[Temple of the Moon in Water]].

## Overview

| Scene | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 1.1 Leaving the Lady | Sandra's parting gift and last words |
| 1.2 Through the Forest | an ordinary, peaceful journey |
| 1.3 The Owlbear Family | a sound in the bushes; avoid the owlbears or fight them |
| 1.4 The Thieves' Camp | thieves attack; the fight can be ended by negotiation |
| 1.5 The Temple | arrival at the Temple of the Moon in Water |

> [!note] Distances
> The temple lies about a day and a half north of the inn through dense forest. The party spends one night in the forest. If the map of the region changes, adjust the travel time.

## 1.1 — Leaving the Lady

The party leaves at first light. The common room is quiet at this hour; the echo-guests have not yet "arrived," and the fire burns without crackling.

> [!quote] Read Aloud
> Sandra is waiting by the door with something small in her hands. In daylight, her disguise is flawless again: warm skin, a tired smile, a shawl pulled around shoulders that cannot feel the cold.
>
> "The map will show you the safest path," she says. "Keep to it. The forest does not forgive people who wander."
>
> She holds out a brass compass. Its needle does not point north. It swings slowly round and settles on her.
>
> "So you can find your way back. I may not be standing where you left me."

**The Lady's Compass.** This small brass compass is a magic item. Its needle always points toward To the Eternal Lady, wherever the inn currently stands, and it works anywhere in the material world. Sandra wants it returned.

If asked, Sandra adds the following:

- Maria Rose's last letter said she was "close to the heart of it."
- Thieves still haunt the roads north. "They are frightened, and frightened people with knives are worse than brave ones."
- "If you find Maria, tell her the kettle is still on." Sandra refuses to explain. It is an old joke between friends.

## 1.2 — Through the Forest

For most of the journey, the forest is simply a forest.

> [!quote] Read Aloud
> Morning light falls in long slanted beams between the trunks. Birds argue in the canopy. A squirrel scolds you from a branch, and somewhere off the path a deer lifts its head, watches you pass, and goes back to grazing.
>
> After the strangeness of the inn, the ordinary forest feels almost like a kindness.

Nothing dangerous happens here. Let the party talk, settle into their marching order, and get to know one another. Describe ordinary wildlife: birds, deer, foxes, a stream crossing, the sounds of the woods.

### Drifting Shards

Along the way, the party sees something strange.

> [!quote] Read Aloud
> Between the trees, a few pieces of something like glass hang in the air at head height. They turn slowly, catching the light. One by one, they vanish—and a moment later, a little farther off, another appears and begins to turn.

The shards are harmless. They cannot be caught; a hand passes through the place where a shard was a moment before.

**Characters from Kagameru** recognize the sight at once. Drifting shards are a normal part of the world wherever the power of [[The Mirrorgate]] bleeds into the material plane. This happens not near mirrors, but near places with a strong connection to the Mirrorgate, such as war-scars and ancient sites like the temple ahead.

**Characters from other worlds** have never seen anything like it. This is a good moment for a native character to explain, or for everyone to discover that the world they have come to is stranger than the one they left.

The shards appear more often as the party nears the temple.

## 1.3 — The Owlbear Family

Later in the day, a sound comes from a dense thicket just off the path: a deep, rumbling hoot, followed by snapping branches and a series of high, chirping squawks.

Any character can make a **DC 13 Intelligence (Nature or History) check** to identify the sounds.

**On a success**, the character recognizes the sounds of an **owlbear family**: two adults and their young, feeding in the thicket. The party can choose what to do:

- **Avoid them.** The party can move on quietly in a wide arc around the thicket without disturbing the family. No check is needed if they keep their distance.
- **Fight them.** The party can attack the owlbears willingly. The adults defend their young to the death.

**On a failure**, the party does not know what is making the sounds.

- **If they move on**, they pass without trouble.
- **If they investigate the thicket**, they stumble into the owlbears' feeding ground, and the adults attack at once. The owlbears have Advantage on their Initiative rolls.

> [!danger] Encounter: The Owlbear Family
> - 2 **Owlbears** (SRD 5.2.1), the parents
> - the owlbear young, which do not fight and flee if the battle turns against their parents
>
> **Difficulty:** 1,400 XP, a Moderate encounter for four 4th-level characters.  
> **Scaling:** For three characters, use one adult owlbear. For five characters, the adults fight with Advantage on their first attack roll each, as they defend their young.

## 1.4 — The Thieves' Camp

The journey goes on. Late in the afternoon, the path passes close to a camp of thieves. There is no check to notice them in time: the thieves have been watching the path, and they attack.

> [!quote] Read Aloud
> A whistle cuts through the trees. Then another, answering it. Figures rise out of the undergrowth on both sides of the path: ragged cloaks, drawn bows, rusted blades.
>
> "Packs on the ground," a voice shouts. "Now!"
>
> They do not wait for an answer.

Roll initiative. The thieves attack immediately.

> [!danger] Encounter: The Thieves
> - 1 **Bandit Captain** (SRD 5.2.1)
> - 4 **Scouts** (SRD 5.2.1), archers in the trees
> - 8 **Bandits** (SRD 5.2.1)
>
> **Difficulty:** 1,050 XP, a Low encounter for four 4th-level characters, but with many attackers.  
> **Scaling:** For three characters, remove two Scouts and two Bandits. For five characters, add two Scouts.

### Negotiating the Fight

The thieves are desperate, not fanatical. Once the fight has begun, the party can try to end it with words. A character can use an action to call out to the thieves and make one of the following checks:

- **DC 15 Charisma (Persuasion):** Convince the captain that the party is not worth dying for.
- **DC 15 Charisma (Intimidation):** Convince the thieves that they are losing.
- **DC 12** for either check if the Bandit Captain is Bloodied or at least half of the thieves have been defeated.

On a success, the captain calls off the attack. The surviving thieves back away into the forest, or stay and talk if the party treats them fairly. On a failure, the fight continues, and another character can try again on their own turn.

If the thieves are willing to talk, they can confirm that the old ruin lies farther north, and warn that "people who go there don't come back the same."

## 1.5 — The Temple

The party spends the night in the forest and walks on the next morning. By late afternoon of the second day, they finally arrive at the temple.

> [!quote] Read Aloud
> The trees open onto a hill crowned with broken stone. Columns lean against one another like tired old men. Moss covers the steps, and roots have split them into pieces.
>
> Above the entrance, a cracked lintel holds a line of old carved letters, worn almost smooth.
>
> The temple is silent. Even the birds have stopped singing.

The party has reached the [[Temple of the Moon in Water]]. The lintel reads *the moon in the water*; a character who succeeds on a **DC 14 Intelligence (History or Religion) check** recognizes the old saying and notices that its other half is missing (see the temple's entry).

## Rewards and Consequences

- **Experience.** If you use milestone levelling, this chapter does not end a level.
- **Treasure.** The thieves carry a total of **3d10 gp** and **5d10 sp**. The Bandit Captain carries a **Potion of Healing**.
- **The Lady's Compass**, on loan from Sandra.
- **The owlbears.** If the party avoided the family rather than fighting it, note it. A later scene may reward their restraint.
