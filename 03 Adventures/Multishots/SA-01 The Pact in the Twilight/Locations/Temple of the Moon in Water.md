# Temple of the Moon in Water

> *“A ruin is only an abandoned place when nothing on the other side remembers it.”*  
> —[[Professor Phineas Phantomhive II]]

The **Temple of the Moon in Water**—to most who know of it, simply *the ruined temple*—stands in the northern reaches of the [[Forest of Thieves]]. It is the site marked on the map given to the adventurers by [[Sandra Mistic]] and the place where her old friend, [[Maria Rose]], investigated the spread of the luminous plants.

The temple is very old and very badly kept. Many of its rooms are still sealed behind the puzzles of a forgotten civilization, and whole corridors have collapsed. In its main hall stands a great mirror that reflects the living correctly—and everything else wrong.

The ruin exists within [[Kagameru]], but it is not confined to Kagameru. Its other half lies inside [[The Mirrorgate]] as the [[Sanctum of the Flower in the Mirror]].

## The Shrine of the Unreachable Bloom

The temple and the sanctum were once a single place: the **Shrine of the Unreachable Bloom**.

It was built by a civilization whose name is now lost, in honour of a **moon god**: one of the Prime Gods destroyed at the end of [[The Primordial War]]. Like the other Prime Gods, its true name was erased. Its body vanished with the rest. Its reflection did not.

The god's followers believed that its reflection survived behind the glass, in the Mirrorgate. They could see it, but never reach it. They prayed to that reflection: in the still water of the shrine, where the moon appeared, and in the great mirror of the main hall, where the god's reflection was said to bloom. The old saying carved over the shrine's entrances describes their faith exactly—*the flower in the mirror, the moon in the water*: things that can be seen but never grasped.

> [!gm] What Happened to the Shrine
> The priests and clerics of the shrine tried to bring their god back. Many of them gathered to perform a great ritual of revival.
>
> It failed drastically. A rift opened and swallowed half of the shrine. That half is now the [[Sanctum of the Flower in the Mirror]]: one of the only places in the Mirrorgate that is **not** a reflection, and one that is strangely disconnected from every other part of the plane.
>
> The rift's power did not vanish. It was absorbed into the **great mirror** in the main hall, which is why the mirror still leads into the sanctum (see [[#The Great Mirror]]).

## The Name

The temple's name survives on a cracked lintel above its entrance as half of the old saying: *the moon in the water*. The other half—*the flower in the mirror*—was carved on the part of the shrine that is no longer here: the [[Sanctum of the Flower in the Mirror]].

A character who reads the inscription and succeeds on a **DC 14 Intelligence (History or Religion) check** recognizes the saying and realizes that its other half is missing.

## Present Condition

The temple is a ruin in every sense. Roots split its floors, rainwater pools in its halls, and nobody has cared for it in centuries.

- **Sealed rooms.** Many chambers are still locked behind puzzles left by the shrine's builders. Their mechanisms are old, but most still work.
- **Collapsed corridors.** Whole passages have fallen in completely and cannot be cleared. The party must find other routes, often through the sealed rooms.
- **Missing architecture.** Some doorways open onto bare forest, and some walls end in clean, straight edges, as though cut away. These are the parts of the shrine that now stand in the Mirrorgate.

## Areas of the Temple

The temple is explored in [[SA-01 Chapter 2]]:

1. **The Entrance Hall** — the ruined first rooms and the lintel
2. **The Courtyard Pool** — where the faithful watched the moon
3. **The Fallen Corridors** — collapsed passages haunted by glass husks
4. **The Moon Door** — a door that opens when its moon is reflected in water
5. **The Hall of Backward Words** — the priests' library, with a prayer that reads correctly only in a mirror
6. **The Seen Door** — a door that opens only for someone watching it in a reflection
7. **The Antechamber** — the last guardians before the hall
8. **The Worship Hall** — the great mirror

## The Courtyard Pool

At the temple's heart lies a still, dark pool. The shrine's faithful once gathered around it at night to watch the moon appear in its water.

## The Great Mirror

A massive mirror stands at the northern end of the worship hall, taller than any door in the temple. Its frame is carved with the same moon and flower that appear on the lintels.

The mirror does not reflect correctly.

> [!quote] Read Aloud
> The mirror shows you exactly as you are: dusty, tired, weapons in hand. But the hall behind you is wrong. In the glass, there are no fallen stones and no roots in the floor. The pillars stand whole, the walls rise higher than they should, and the doorways open onto rooms you did not pass on your way in.
>
> You turn around. Behind you, the hall is still a ruin.

The mirror reflects living creatures correctly, but behind them it shows the [[Sanctum of the Flower in the Mirror]]. A creature that steps into the mirror passes through it and arrives in the sanctum.

### Why the Mirror Is Here

In the faith of the moon god, everyone who prayed—the priests included—had to look toward the moon, which from this temple was always in the north. The priest therefore stood behind the crowd. The great mirror let newcomers, and anyone else who needed to see the priest, watch them in the glass without turning their back on the moon.

### The Mirror's Power

The power of the rift that swallowed half of the shrine is now held inside the great mirror. Nobody knows how it works.

**Overwhelming Magic.** When a creature uses *Detect Magic*, or a similar feature that senses magic (such as a Wild Magic barbarian's Magic Awareness), within 30 feet of the great mirror, it learns nothing about any spell, school of magic, or magic item. It is simply overwhelmed by the mirror's power.

**Blinding Light.** If the creature can see the great mirror while it senses magic, it must make a **DC 15 Constitution saving throw**. *Failure:* The creature has the Blinded condition for 1 minute. It repeats the save at the end of each of its turns, ending the effect on itself on a success.

## Inhabitants

The temple's halls are haunted by strange figures that look like zombies but are not. Use the [[Glass Husk]] stat block.

Glass husks and other [[Shardkin]] almost never appear in the material world. Why they walk these halls is part of the shrine's mystery.

## Side Quest: The Disconnected Shrine

> [!info] Quest
> **Goal:** Find out what happened to the Shrine of the Unreachable Bloom, and why it feels so disconnected from the real world.

**Questions the party can investigate:**

- Why is half of the temple missing, cut away as if by a blade?
- Why does the great mirror show a different hall, and why does it overwhelm anyone who senses its magic?
- Why do glass husks walk in the material world, where shardkin almost never appear?
- Why is the sanctum behind the mirror not a reflection, and why can no other part of the Mirrorgate reach it?

**The truth** is described in *What Happened to the Shrine* above: a failed ritual to revive the moon god opened a rift that swallowed half of the shrine, and the rift's power was absorbed into the great mirror. The clues include **diaries and handbook entries** left by the shrine's people, found in the temple and the sanctum; their contents are not yet written. Players who miss them are not punished: [[Maria Rose]] explains the shrine's history when the party meets her in the sanctum.
