# Temple of the Moon in Water

> [!quote] Professor Phineas Phantomhive II
> *"A ruin is only an abandoned place when nothing on the other side remembers it."*

> [!location] At a glance
> - **Kind** Ruined shrine, half of it missing
> - **Where** Northern third of the [[Forest of Thieves]], 25–35 km (15–22 miles) north of [[To the Eternal Lady]], on slightly higher ground beside a spring
> - **Other half** The [[Sanctum of the Flower in the Mirror]], inside [[The Mirrorgate]]
> - **Inhabitants** [[Glass Husk|Glass husks]]

## Overview

The **Temple of the Moon in Water**, to most who know of it simply *the ruined temple*, is the place marked on the map [[Sandra Mistic]] gives the adventurers, and the place where her friend [[Maria Rose]] went to study the glowing plants. It is very old and very badly kept: roots split its floors, rain pools in its halls, many rooms are still sealed behind the puzzles of a forgotten people, and whole corridors have fallen in.

Some doorways open onto bare forest, and some walls end in clean, straight edges, as though cut away. Those are the parts of the temple that are no longer in [[Kagameru]].

## The Shrine of the Unreachable Bloom

The temple and the sanctum were once one place: the **Shrine of the Unreachable Bloom**. A people whose name is lost built it to honour a **moon god**, one of the Prime Gods destroyed at the end of [[The Primordial War]]. Like the other Prime Gods, its true name was erased and its body vanished. Its reflection did not.

The faithful believed the god's reflection survived behind the glass, in the Mirrorgate, where they could see it but never reach it. They prayed to it in the still water of the courtyard pool, where the moon appeared, and in the great mirror of the worship hall, where the god's reflection was said to bloom. The saying carved over the entrances describes their faith exactly: *the flower in the mirror, the moon in the water*, things that can be seen but never grasped.

> [!secret] What happened to the shrine
> The priests tried to bring their god back with a great ritual. It failed. A rift opened and swallowed half of the shrine, which became the [[Sanctum of the Flower in the Mirror]]: one of the only places in the Mirrorgate that is **not** a reflection, and cut off from every other part of the plane. The rift's power was absorbed into the great mirror, which is why the mirror still leads into the sanctum.

## The name

The temple's name survives on a cracked lintel above the entrance as half of the old saying: *the moon in the water*. The other half, *the flower in the mirror*, was carved on the part of the shrine that is no longer here.

> [!rule] Reading the lintel
> A character who reads the inscription and succeeds on an **Intelligence (History or Religion) check, DC 14**, recognises the saying and realises its other half is missing.

## The great mirror

A mirror taller than any door in the temple stands at the northern end of the worship hall. Its frame is carved with the moon and flower from the lintels. In the faith of the moon god, everyone who prayed faced the moon, which from here was always in the north; the priest stood behind the crowd, and the mirror let the faithful see the priest without turning their backs on the moon.

The mirror reflects living creatures correctly, but behind them it shows the sanctum.

> [!read-aloud]
> The mirror shows you exactly as you are: dusty, tired, weapons in hand. But the hall behind you is wrong. In the glass there are no fallen stones and no roots in the floor. The pillars stand whole, the walls rise higher than they should, and the doorways open onto rooms you did not pass on your way in. You turn around. Behind you, the hall is still a ruin.

A creature that steps into the mirror passes through it and arrives in the [[Sanctum of the Flower in the Mirror]].

> [!rule] The mirror's power
> **Overwhelming Magic.** A creature that casts *Detect Magic*, or uses any other feature that senses magic, within 30 feet of the great mirror learns nothing about any spell, school of magic or magic item; the mirror's power drowns everything out.
>
> **Blinding Light.** If that creature can see the mirror while sensing magic, it makes a **Constitution saving throw, DC 15**. *Failure:* it has the Blinded condition for 1 minute; it repeats the save at the end of each of its turns, ending the effect on itself on a success.

## Side quest: The Disconnected Shrine

> [!quest] The Disconnected Shrine
> **Goal:** find out what happened to the Shrine of the Unreachable Bloom, and why it feels so cut off from the world.
> - [ ] Why is half of the temple missing, cut away as if by a blade?
> - [ ] Why does the great mirror show a different hall?
> - [ ] Why do glass husks walk in the material world, where shardkin almost never appear?
> - [ ] Why is the sanctum not a reflection, and why can no other part of the Mirrorgate reach it?

Clues are diaries and handbook entries left by the shrine's people, found in the temple and the sanctum. Players who miss them are not punished: [[Maria Rose]] tells the shrine's history when the party meets her in the sanctum.

> [!gm]- Open mysteries
> - What was the lost people that built the shrine, and what became of them?
> - What became of the priests who performed the ritual?
> - Who were the glass husks, and why are they here?
> - Why are the luminous plants spreading from the ruin?
> - Is the temple connected to the artifact Sandra guards?

> [!player] What players know
> An old ruined temple stands in the north of the Forest of Thieves, beside a spring. Sandra Mistic's map marks it, and her friend Maria Rose went there to study the glowing plants. Half the saying carved over its door survives: *the moon in the water*.
