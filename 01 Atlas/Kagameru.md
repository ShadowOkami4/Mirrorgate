# Kagameru

> [!quote] Professor Phineas Phantomhive II
> *“Every world believes itself ordinary until one reads its footnotes.”*

**Kagameru** is the central world of the Mirrored Realms: a land of old scars, unfinished histories, contested relics, and reflections that do not always obey their owners.

Its peoples build kingdoms, roads, temples, academies, inns, and border forts atop the remains of powers far older than their maps. Beneath those foundations lie ruins from the age of [[The Primordial War]], wounds where time and space still bleed, and doors into places no sensible traveler would call safe.

## The name

*Kagameru* (*kah-gah-MEH-roo*) grows from an old-tongue root meaning *mirror*. The Encyclopedia Kagami is named after the same old-tongue root as the world.

## Defining features

- The world was shaped by the legendary [[The Primordial War]].
- The [[Scars of the Primordial War]] remain one of its greatest sources of wonder, danger, and contradiction.
- The present age is increasingly known as [[The Shattered Hope Era]].
- Certain mirrors and planar wounds connect Kagameru to [[The Mirrorgate]].
- Time, space, reflection, and memory are central forces in its oldest mysteries.
- It stands between the Middle Ages and the early Industrial Revolution: crowns and castles, but also railways and early firearms. See [[Technology and Daily Life]].
- Its peoples and cultures are recorded in [[Peoples]].
- Its lands and political powers are gathered in the [[Atlas]].

## Current understanding

Kagameru is not a fully settled or fully understood world. Many of its maps are incomplete, many of its histories contradict each other, and many of its ancient sites have been sealed for excellent reasons that modern explorers are determined to ignore.

## Campaign use

Kagameru is designed to support campaigns about:

- relic-hunting and ruin exploration
- political conflict over ancient war-scars
- divine mysteries and lost gods
- mirror-world travel, unstable realities, and broken chronology
- noble houses with dangerous histories
- ordinary people trying to survive extraordinary forces

## Continents

The surviving atlas fragments of [[Kagameru]] name six great landmasses. Scholars have long noticed something curious about these names: in the oldest tongues of the world, every one of them means *mirror*. Whether the first cartographers were poets, pessimists, or simply well informed remains a matter of debate.

### The Southern Ice

Newer maps show a seventh landmass that the old atlas fragments do not name: a vast, icebound land in the far south. For now it is recorded simply as the **Southern Ice**. It has no old-tongue name, and nothing is yet known of its peoples or history.

> [!warning] Provisional
> The continents are working references. The current world map places them, but their borders and peoples may still change, and the Southern Ice has not yet been given a name or a place in the lore.

### Tensions across the map

Even incomplete records show recurring causes of conflict:

- competition for ruins caught in broken time
- noble claims based on half-remembered bloodlines
- religious disputes over visible scars of [[The Primordial War]]
- expeditions vanishing near mirror-linked sites
- border conflicts sparked by newly uncovered ruins

## On names

The world, its continents, its kingdoms, and its great cities carry names grown from old-tongue roots for four things: *mirror*, *reflection*, *time*, and *space*. The roots come from many tongues, and none survives unchanged: each has been worn down by centuries of speech into something that sounds like a place rather than a word. The grander and older the land, the closer it stays to its root; villages often keep only a fragment of one, joined to a plain ending.

Forests, inns, roads, and other local landmarks are known by their common-tongue names, such as the [[Forest of Thieves]] and [[To the Eternal Lady]]. Places within one realm tend to share the flavour of one old tongue, which is often the quickest way to guess where a traveler comes from.

See the [[Pronunciation Guide]] for how to say them.

## Political themes

- Rival powers competing over access to [[Scars of the Primordial War]]
- Noble houses preserving partial records of pre-modern history
- Borderlands where impossible ruins matter more than capitals
- Scholars, churches, and rulers disagreeing over who has the right to study ancient war-scars
- Hidden routes connected to [[The Mirrorgate]]
- Crown propaganda in [[Lustro]] denying that the Mirrorgate exists
- [[The Unsilvered]] working against royal control to regain freedom
