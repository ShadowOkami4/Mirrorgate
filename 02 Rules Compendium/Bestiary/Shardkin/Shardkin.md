# Shardkin

> *"A broken mirror does not stop reflecting. It merely becomes less polite about what it shows."*  
> —[[Professor Phineas Phantomhive II]]

**Shardkin** are creatures made from broken reflections. Some were once people who met the wrong mirror. Others were never people at all, only images, moments, or echoes that learned to move without an owner.

They gather wherever reflection, time, or distance has been damaged: beside cracked mirrors, in ruins where the hours run wrong, and near crossings into [[The Mirrorgate]].

Shardkin are not the natives of the Mirrorgate. The plane has living plants and animals of its own, and most of them avoid shardkin as carefully as travelers do. See [[Life in the Mirrorgate]].

## One Person, Three Monsters

Scholars have noticed something grim about the most common shardkin: a person shattered by a mirror rarely becomes *one* monster. They break apart.

- The **body** is left behind, hollow, and something jagged moves into the space where the soul used to be. This is a [[Glass Husk]].
- The **soul** splinters into small, lonely lights that drift in search of anyone warm. These are [[Glint|Glints]].
- The **self**—the part that remembers a name, a face, and a life—survives as a [[Remnant]], and it hates everyone who is still whole.

A husk, a handful of glints, and a remnant found together are usually the pieces of the same unfortunate person.

## Born from Reflections

Other shardkin never belonged to anyone. They are what reflections become when the glass breaks and nothing tells them to stop.

- An [[Afterimage]] is a moment that got left behind, living two seconds behind the rest of the world.
- A [[Counterfeit]] is a reflection that stepped out of its mirror and decided it would make a better version of its original.
- A [[Kaleidoscope]] is a whirling mass of mirror fragments that holds every reflection ever lost in a place.

## Signs of Shardkin

- mirrors fogging, cracking, or showing the creature before it appears
- reflections that are a moment late, or missing entirely
- voices that mimic the last person the creature harmed
- memories leaking into nearby dreams
- glittering dust that never settles

## Running Shardkin

Shardkin work best when they feel tragic as well as dangerous. They are not ordinary beasts; they are what remains when someone is broken by the wrong reflection.

- **Let the pieces tell a story.** Players who realize that a husk, its glints, and a remnant were once the same person should find a name, a portrait, or a diary nearby.
- **Use the mirrors.** A room with mirrors should be more dangerous than one without them: counterfeits step out of them, and remnants watch through them.
- **Encounter example.** One [[Remnant]], one [[Glass Husk]], and two [[Glint|Glints]] (750 XP) make a moderate encounter for four 3rd-level characters.

## Reading the Stat Blocks

Stat blocks in the Mirrored Realms follow the fifth-edition format of the SRD 5.2.1: armor class and initiative first, a combined ability and saving-throw table, a single **Immunities** line for damage types and conditions, and actions split into Traits, Actions, Bonus Actions, and Reactions.

## Further Ideas

Shardkin not yet given stat blocks:

- **Glint Swarm** — hundreds of glints; what remains when an entire village is shattered at once.
- **Hour Moths** — pale moths that feed on seconds. Where they swarm, candles burn backward and wounds reopen.
- **Quicksilver Eel** — lives in still, mirror-bright pools. Its bite steals a creature's reflection, and the reflection swims away.
- **Frame Warden** — an ornate, empty mirror frame that drifts through a ruin and swallows anyone who looks through it.
- **Stillborn Moment** — a frozen scene of people caught mid-gesture. It is perfectly harmless until someone touches it.
- **The Unfinished** — a creature that exists partly in the next moment: its claws strike a round after it swings.
