# Kaleidoscope

> *"In each shard I saw a different face, and every one of them had once stood where I was standing. I left before it could add mine."*
> —[[Professor Phineas Phantomhive II]]

A **kaleidoscope** is a slowly turning storm of broken mirror fragments, held together by nothing but reflected light. It forms in places where many mirrors have shattered—abandoned halls, collapsed palaces, the workshops of careless enchanters—and it gathers into itself every reflection that was ever lost there.

Looking into a kaleidoscope is unsettling. Each fragment shows a different face: past visitors, long-dead residents, and occasionally the viewer, arriving a few seconds from now. It chimes softly as it turns, like a hall of wind bells, and its light can overwhelm the mind of anyone who stares too long. Blunt force is its weakness; a hammer does what no sword can.

### Traits

**Every Angle.** The kaleidoscope can't be surprised, and it can see Invisible creatures and objects within 60 feet of itself.

### Actions

**Multiattack.** The kaleidoscope makes three Shard Lash attacks.

**Shard Lash.** *Melee Attack Roll:* +6, reach 10 ft. *Hit:* 12 (2d8 + 3) Slashing damage.

**Kaleidoscopic Flash (Recharge 5–6).** *Wisdom Saving Throw:* DC 15, each creature in a 30-foot Cone. *Failure:* 21 (6d6) Psychic damage, and the target has the Blinded condition until the end of its next turn. *Success:* Half damage only.

### Bonus Actions

**Refract.** The kaleidoscope teleports up to 30 feet to an unoccupied space it can see.

### Reactions

**Reflect Spell.** *Trigger:* A spell attack roll misses the kaleidoscope. *Response:* The kaleidoscope reflects the spell at another creature it can see within 60 feet of itself. The caster makes a new attack roll with the spell against that creature.

## Running Kaleidoscopes

A kaleidoscope is a boss for a room full of broken mirrors. It refracts to wherever it can catch the most characters in its Flash and lashes anyone who closes in. Let the party hear the glass crack loudly whenever it takes bludgeoning damage, so they discover its weakness in play. Its shards can show the faces of people the party is searching for—a way to deliver clues in the middle of a fight.
